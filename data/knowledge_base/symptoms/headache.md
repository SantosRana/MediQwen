# Headaches

## Overview
- Most headaches are not serious and resolve on their own.
- Duration can range from 30 minutes to several days.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Drink plenty of water
- Rest if you also have a cold or flu
- Relax (stress can worsen headaches)
- Take paracetamol or ibuprofen
- Stay at home if you have a high temperature or feel unwell

**Don’t:**
- Skip meals
- Oversleep (can worsen headaches)
- Strain eyes for long periods (e.g., screen use)
- Drink alcohol

## When to See a GP
See a GP if:
- Headache does not improve or worsens despite self-care
- Headaches occur regularly

## Urgent GP / NHS 111
Seek urgent help if:
- Headache with vision or eye problems
- Triggered/worsened by coughing, sneezing, bending, or exercise
- Headache with vomiting
- Child’s headache worsening or waking them at night
- Headache with jaw pain when eating or sore/tender scalp
- Any other worrying symptoms

## Emergency (999 / A&E)
Call emergency services if headache is accompanied by:
- Seizure (fit)
- Numbness or weakness in body/face
- Sudden, extremely painful onset
- Recent head injury (within 3 months)
- Difficulty speaking, balancing, walking, or memory issues
- Drowsiness or confusion
- Loss of vision
- Rash that does not fade with glass test
- Very high temperature, stiff neck, sensitivity to light

**Note:** Do not drive to A&E. Bring current medicines with you.

## Causes
Common causes:
- Cold or flu
- Stress
- Alcohol
- Poor posture
- Eyesight problems
- Skipping meals
- Dehydration
- Overuse of painkillers
- Hormonal changes (period, menopause)

Severe headaches may be due to:
- Migraine
- Cluster headache

## Notes
- Keeping a headache diary can help identify triggers.
- Most headaches are benign, but persistent or severe cases require medical evaluation.
