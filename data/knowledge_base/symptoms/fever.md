# High Temperature (Fever)

## Adults

### What is a High Temperature?
- Normal body temperature varies by person and time of day.
- A high temperature (fever) is usually **38°C or above**.

### How to Check
You may have a high temperature if:
- Chest or back feels hotter than usual
- Other symptoms: feeling hot or cold, shivery (chills), sweating, skin warm/red (may be harder to see on darker skin)
- Thermometer reading of 38°C or above

**Important:** You may still have a fever if you feel hot, cold, or shivery even when thermometer reads below 38°C.

### Taking Your Temperature
- Digital thermometers are recommended.
- Digital ear thermometers: gently pull ear up/back before use.
- Avoid: glass thermometers (dangerous), some infrared thermometers (not regulated), forehead strips (inaccurate).

### Self Care
- Rest
- Drink plenty of fluids (water best) → urine should be light yellow/clear
- Take paracetamol or ibuprofen if uncomfortable
- Stay at home and avoid contact until fever subsides

### When to Seek Medical Help
- Ask for urgent GP appointment or NHS 111 if:
  - Fever persists despite home treatment
  - Fever is getting worse

### Causes
- Often due to body fighting infection (cold, flu).
- Usually not serious.
- Can occasionally signal something more serious if very high or persistent.

## Children

### What is a High Temperature?
- Very common in young children.
- Usually returns to normal within 1–4 days.
- A high temperature is **38°C or more**.

### Signs
Child may:
- Feel hotter than usual (back/chest)
- Feel sweaty
- Look or feel unwell
- Have a seizure/fit (febrile seizure)

### Taking Temperature
- Use a digital thermometer (pharmacy/supermarket).
- Avoid glass, infrared, or forehead strip thermometers.

### Self Care
**Do:**
- Give plenty of fluids (continue breastfeeding if applicable)
- Watch for dehydration
- Offer food if wanted
- Check child regularly, including at night
- Keep child at home
- Give paracetamol or ibuprofen if distressed/uncomfortable (check suitability with pharmacist/GP)

**Don’t:**
- Do not undress or sponge down to cool
- Do not overdress or use too many bedclothes
- Do not give aspirin under age 16
- Do not alternate ibuprofen/paracetamol unless advised by doctor
- Do not give paracetamol under 2 months
- Do not give ibuprofen under 3 months, <5kg, with chickenpox, or dehydrated
- Do not give ibuprofen to children with asthma unless doctor recommends

### When to Seek Medical Help
Call **111 or GP** if child:
- Under 3 months with temp ≥38°C
- 3–6 months with temp ≥39°C
- Has rash with fever
- Fever lasts ≥5 days
- Not eating, not themselves, or you’re worried
- Signs of dehydration (dry nappies, sunken eyes, no tears)

Call **999 immediately** if child:
- Has stiff neck
- Rash that does not fade with glass test
- Sensitivity to light
- First febrile seizure
- Cold hands/feet, skin color changes (blue, grey, pale, blotchy), lips/tongue pale/blue
- Drowsy, hard to wake, extremely agitated or confused
- Difficulty breathing (grunting, stomach sucking under ribs, very fast breathing)
- Not responding normally or uninterested in feeding/activities

### Causes
- Natural response to fighting infections (coughs, colds).
- Common childhood illnesses (chickenpox, tonsillitis).
- Can also occur after vaccinations.

---

## Notes
- Fever is a **natural immune response** in both adults and children.  
- Most cases resolve with self-care, but persistent or severe fever requires medical attention.  
- Children have stricter thresholds for emergency care due to higher risk of serious illness.
