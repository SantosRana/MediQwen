# Diarrhoea and Vomiting

## Overview
- Common in adults, children, and babies.
- Often caused by a stomach bug.
- Usually resolves within a few days.
- Advice is the same whether diarrhoea and vomiting occur together or separately.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Stay at home and rest
- Drink plenty of fluids (small sips if nauseous)
- Continue breast/bottle feeding babies; give smaller, more frequent feeds if vomiting
- Offer babies on formula/solids small sips of water between feeds
- Eat when able; avoid fatty or spicy foods
- Take paracetamol for discomfort (check leaflet before giving to children)

**Don’t:**
- Avoid fruit juice or fizzy drinks (worsens diarrhoea)
- Do not dilute baby formula
- Do not give children under 12 anti-diarrhoea medicine
- Do not give aspirin to children under 16

## Duration
- Diarrhoea: usually stops within 5–7 days
- Vomiting: usually stops within 1–2 days

## Preventing Spread
- Stay off school/work until symptom-free for 2 days
- Wash hands with soap and water frequently
- Wash contaminated clothing/bedding separately on hot wash
- Clean toilets, flush handles, taps, surfaces, door handles daily
- Avoid preparing food for others
- Do not share towels, flannels, cutlery, utensils
- Avoid swimming pools until 48 hours after symptoms stop

## Pharmacist Help
Pharmacists may recommend:
- Oral rehydration powder (mixed with water)
- Medicine to stop diarrhoea for a few hours (e.g., loperamide; not for children <12)

Seek pharmacist advice if:
- Signs of dehydration (dark/smelly urine, reduced urination)
- Older age, weakened immune system, or other health conditions increasing dehydration risk
- Need to temporarily stop diarrhoea

## When to Seek Medical Help
**Call 111 if:**
- Concern about baby <12 months
- Child stops feeding
- Child <5 shows dehydration (fewer wet nappies)
- Signs of dehydration persist despite oral rehydration
- Persistent vomiting (cannot keep fluids down)
- Bloody diarrhoea or bleeding from bottom
- Diarrhoea >7 days or vomiting >2 days

**Call 999 / A&E if:**
- Vomiting blood or vomit looks like ground coffee
- Green vomit (adults) / yellow-green or green vomit (children)
- Possible poisoning
- Stiff neck + pain with bright lights
- Sudden, severe headache or tummy ache
- Skin/lips/tongue blue, grey, pale, blotchy (check palms/soles in darker skin)
- Severe breathing difficulty or rapid, shallow breathing
- Confusion or not responding normally

**Note:** Do not drive to A&E. Bring current medicines with you.

## Causes
Most common causes:
- Stomach bug
- Food poisoning

## Notes
- Usually self-limiting but can spread easily.
- Hydration is the most important factor.
- Seek urgent help if symptoms persist, worsen, or involve blood/serious signs.
