# Stress

## Overview
- Stress is a common response to mental or emotional pressure.
- Can sometimes be motivating, but persistent stress can harm wellbeing.
- Support and self-help strategies are available.

## Symptoms
Stress can affect:
- **Physical:** faster heartbeat, sweating, headaches, muscle tension
- **Mental:** anxiety, irritability, difficulty concentrating
- **Behavioural:** changes in sleep, appetite, social withdrawal, reliance on unhealthy coping mechanisms

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Talk about feelings with friends, family, or professionals
- Use stress-busting techniques (exercise, relaxation, time for yourself)
- Practice time-management strategies
- Try breathing exercises
- Plan ahead for stressful events
- Consider peer support groups
- Listen to free mental wellbeing audio guides

**Don’t:**
- Avoid trying to do everything at once
- Do not dwell on things you cannot change
- Avoid isolation — most people experience stress
- Do not use alcohol, cigarettes, gambling, or drugs to cope

## When to Seek Help
See a GP if:
- Stress is overwhelming or coping strategies are not helping
- You prefer a referral for therapy

**NHS Talking Therapies:**
- Available for adults (18+, or 16+ in some areas)
- Self-referral possible without GP

**Urgent GP / NHS 111:**
- If urgent help is needed but not an emergency

**Emergency (999 / A&E):**
- If immediate help is required
- If someone has seriously harmed themselves (e.g., overdose)

## Causes
Stress often arises from:
- **Work:** pressure, unemployment, retirement
- **Family:** relationship difficulties, divorce, caregiving
- **Financial:** bills, debt
- **Health:** illness, injury, bereavement

Even positive life events (buying a house, having a baby, planning a wedding) can trigger stress.

## Notes
- Stress hormones (adrenaline, cortisol) prepare the body for action.
- Short-term stress can be helpful, but chronic stress is harmful.
- Identifying causes makes management easier.
