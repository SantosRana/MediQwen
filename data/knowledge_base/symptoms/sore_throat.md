# Sore Throat

## Overview
- Very common and usually not serious.
- Typically resolves within a week without treatment.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Gargle with warm, salty water (not for children)
- Drink plenty of water
- Eat cool or soft foods
- Avoid smoking or smoky places
- Suck ice cubes, lollies, or hard sweets (not for young children due to choking risk)
- Rest

**Don’t:**
- Avoid skipping meals
- Avoid oversleeping (can worsen headache associated with sore throat)
- Avoid straining eyes for long periods
- Avoid alcohol

## Pharmacist Help
Pharmacists can advise on:
- Pain relief (paracetamol, ibuprofen)
- Medicated lozenges (local anaesthetic, antiseptic, anti-inflammatory)
- Anaesthetic sprays (limited evidence of benefit)

**Note:** Always check suitability of medicines (e.g., pregnancy).

## Antibiotics
- Not usually needed for sore throats.
- Only prescribed if bacterial infection suspected.

## When to See a GP
See a GP if:
- Child under 5 needs help
- Symptoms persist >1 week
- Frequent sore throats
- Sore throat with lump in mouth/neck or mouth ulcer >3 weeks

## Urgent GP / NHS 111
Seek urgent help if sore throat with:
- Very high temperature, feeling hot/cold/shivery
- Signs of dehydration (dark urine, reduced urination)
- Weakened immune system (e.g., diabetes, chemotherapy)
- Worrying symptoms

## Emergency (999 / A&E)
Call emergency services if sore throat with:
- Difficulty breathing or swallowing
- Drooling (unable to swallow)
- High-pitched breathing sound (stridor)
- Severe, rapidly worsening symptoms

**Note:** Do not drive to A&E. Bring current medicines with you.

## Symptoms
Typical signs:
- Painful throat, especially when swallowing
- Dry, scratchy throat
- Redness in back of mouth
- Bad breath
- Mild cough
- Swollen neck glands

Children may also have:
- High temperature
- Reduced activity

## Causes
Most sore throats caused by:
- Viral infections (cold, flu)
- Smoking

Other causes:
- Laryngitis
- Tonsillitis
- Strep throat (bacterial)
- Glandular fever

## Notes
- Most sore throats are mild and self-limiting.
- Antibiotics rarely needed.
- Persistent or severe symptoms require medical evaluation.
