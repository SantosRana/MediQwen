# Toothache

## Overview
- Toothache is common and often caused by dental problems.
- Usually requires dental treatment if lasting >2 days.
- Self-care can ease pain while waiting for an appointment.

## When to See a Dentist
See a dentist if toothache:
- Lasts more than 2 days
- Does not improve with painkillers
- Accompanied by high temperature, pain when biting, red gums, or bad taste
- Causes cheek or jaw swelling

**Note:** GP cannot provide dental treatment.

## Emergency (999 / A&E)
Go to A&E if toothache with:
- Swelling around eye or neck
- Swelling in mouth/neck causing difficulty breathing, swallowing, or speaking

**Note:** Do not drive yourself. Bring current medicines with you.

## Accessing Dental Care
- Call your dentist (answerphone may give instructions if closed)
- If no dentist or cannot get emergency appointment:
  - Call 111 or use 111 online
  - Find a nearby dentist and request emergency appointment
- NHS dental charges may apply

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Take painkillers (ibuprofen, paracetamol; no aspirin for under 16s)
- Use pain-relieving mouth gel
- Rinse mouth with salt water (not for children)
- Eat soft foods, avoid chewing with sore tooth
- Use soft toothbrush, avoid flossing around sore tooth

**Don’t:**
- Avoid sweet, very hot, or very cold foods
- Do not smoke (worsens dental problems)

**Salt Water Rinse:**
- Dissolve ½ teaspoon salt in warm water
- Rinse mouth, spit out (do not swallow)
- Repeat as needed

## Causes
Toothache may be caused by:
- Tooth decay
- Dental abscess
- Cracked/damaged tooth
- Loose/broken filling
- Infection (e.g., wisdom tooth eruption)
- Problems with braces
- Gum disease
- Teeth grinding
- Sensitive teeth

## Prevention
- Regular dental check-ups
- Reduce sugary foods/drinks (only occasional treats at mealtimes)
- Brush teeth twice daily (2 minutes, fluoride toothpaste)
- Clean between teeth daily (floss or interdental brush)

## Notes
- Toothache often signals underlying dental issues.
- Early dental treatment prevents complications.
- Self-care can ease pain but does not replace treatment.
