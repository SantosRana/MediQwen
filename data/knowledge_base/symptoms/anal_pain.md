# Anal Pain

## Common Causes
Anal pain has many possible causes. Do not self-diagnose. Examples:

| Symptom Pattern | Possible Cause |
|-----------------|----------------|
| Pooing less than normal, hard poo, pain when pooing | Constipation |
| Itchy anus, lump around anus, blood after wiping | Piles (haemorrhoids) |
| Sharp pain when pooing, burning pain after, blood after wiping | Anal fissures |
| Constant throbbing pain, pus, blood, high temperature | Anal fistulas or abscesses |

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Drink plenty of fluids, eat more fibre
- Exercise regularly
- Wipe with damp toilet paper
- Take paracetamol
- Warm baths to ease itching/pain
- Ice pack (wrapped) for 20 minutes every 2–3 hours

**Don’t:**
- Scratch around anus
- Ignore urge to poo
- Push too hard when pooing
- Use fragranced products around anus
- Eat spicy food, drink alcohol or caffeine
- Lift/carry heavy items

## Pharmacist Help
Pharmacists can recommend:
- Painkillers (ibuprofen, paracetamol)
- Creams or suppositories for itching, pain, swelling
- Laxatives to ease constipation

## When to See a Doctor
See a GP if:
- Pain is severe
- Pain does not improve after a few days
- Blood in poo or bleeding from bottom

**Urgent GP / NHS 111:**
- Severe anal pain with high temperature, feeling hot/cold/shivery
- Black or dark red poo

**A&E / 999:**
- Continuous bleeding from bottom
- Large amounts of blood (toilet water turns red, large clots)

## Treatments
Treatment depends on cause. GP may prescribe:
- Creams or ointments
- Laxatives
- Antibiotics (if infection present)

GP may also recommend dietary changes or arrange further tests.  
In severe cases (fissures, piles, fistulas), surgery or other treatments may be required.

## Notes
- Anal pain is common and treatable.
- Do not feel embarrassed to seek medical help.
- Early treatment prevents complications.
