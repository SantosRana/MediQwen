# Insomnia

## Overview
- Insomnia means regularly having problems sleeping.
- Often improves with changes to sleeping habits.
- **Short-term insomnia:** lasts <3 months  
- **Long-term insomnia:** lasts ≥3 months

## Symptoms
You may have insomnia if you regularly:
- Find it hard to fall asleep
- Wake up several times during the night
- Lie awake at night
- Wake up early and cannot return to sleep
- Still feel tired after waking
- Struggle to nap despite tiredness
- Feel tired/irritable during the day
- Find it difficult to concentrate due to tiredness

## Sleep Needs
- Adults: 7–9 hours
- Children: 9–13 hours
- Toddlers/babies: 12–17 hours  
Constant daytime tiredness suggests insufficient sleep.

## Causes
Common causes:
- Stress, anxiety, depression
- Noise
- Room too hot/cold
- Uncomfortable bed
- Alcohol, caffeine, nicotine
- Illegal drugs (e.g., cocaine, ecstasy)
- Jet lag
- Shift work

Other medical conditions can also contribute.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Go to bed only when sleepy
- Wake up/get out of bed at same time daily
- Relax 1 hour before bed (bath, book)
- Ensure bedroom is dark/quiet (curtains, blinds, mask, ear plugs)
- Exercise regularly during the day
- Use comfortable mattress, pillows, covers

**Don’t:**
- Avoid smoking, alcohol, tea, coffee ≥6 hours before bed
- Avoid large meals late at night
- Avoid exercise within 4 hours of bedtime
- Avoid screens before bed (blue light keeps you awake)
- Do not nap during the day
- Do not drive when sleepy
- Do not oversleep after a bad night — keep regular hours

## Pharmacist Help
Pharmacy sleep aids may include:
- Natural ingredients (valerian, lavender)
- Antihistamine-based products

Notes:
- May help for 1–2 weeks only
- Not a cure for insomnia
- Possible side effects (e.g., drowsiness affecting driving)
- Check with doctor before use

## When to See a GP
Seek medical advice if:
- Sleep habit changes do not help
- Insomnia persists for months
- Insomnia severely affects daily life

## GP Treatments
- GP will investigate underlying causes
- Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) often recommended (face-to-face or online)
- Referral to sleep clinic if another disorder suspected (e.g., sleep apnoea)
- Sleeping pills rarely prescribed due to side effects and dependency risk
- Pills only prescribed short-term if insomnia is severe and other treatments fail

## Notes
- Insomnia is common and usually manageable with lifestyle changes.
- Persistent insomnia requires medical evaluation.
- CBT is the preferred long-term treatment.
