# Rashes in Babies and Children

## Overview
- Rashes are common in babies and children.
- Often harmless, but some can signal serious illness.
- Parents should trust their judgement if a child seems unwell.

## Emergency (999 / A&E)
Call immediately if child has rash with:
- Stiff neck
- Sensitivity to light
- Confusion, agitation, or unresponsiveness
- Breathing difficulty (grunting, fast breathing, stomach sucking under ribs)
- Pale, blue, grey, or blotchy skin/lips/tongue
- Sudden swelling of lips, mouth, throat, or tongue
- Tight throat or swallowing difficulty
- Rash resembling bruises/bleeding under skin that does not fade with glass test

**Note:** On darker skin, check palms, soles, lips, tongue, eyelids.

## Urgent GP / NHS 111
Seek urgent help if child:
- Has rash you’re worried about
- Feels hotter than usual, sweaty, or shivery
- Under 3 months with temp ≥38°C
- Aged 3–6 months with temp ≥39°C
- Aged ≥5 years with temp <36°C

## Common Rash Types

### Rash with High Temperature
- **Slapped cheek syndrome:** Red rash on cheeks, fever, runny nose, sore throat, headache. Usually treated at home.
- **Hand, foot and mouth disease:** Blisters on hands/feet, mouth ulcers. Usually treated at home.
- **Scarlet fever:** Rough, sandpaper-like rash. GP consultation needed.
- **Measles:** Spotty rash starting on head/neck, spreading to body. GP consultation needed.

### Rash with Itching
- **Heat rash (prickly heat):** Small, itchy, prickly spots. Usually treated at home.
- **Eczema:** Dry, cracked, itchy skin (common behind knees, elbows, neck). GP consultation needed.
- **Hives (allergic reaction):** Raised, itchy patches/spots. Usually treated at home. Call 999 if swelling around mouth or breathing difficulty.
- **Ringworm:** Itchy, ring-shaped patches. Usually treated at home.
- **Chickenpox:** Small, itchy spots → blisters → scabs. Usually treated at home.
- **Impetigo:** Sores/blisters that burst, leaving crusty patches. GP consultation needed.
- **Scabies:** Very itchy raised spots, burrows in skin. Usually treated at home.

### Rash without Fever or Itching
- **Milia:** Tiny white/yellow spots on baby’s face. Harmless, resolves naturally.
- **Erythema toxicum:** Red/yellow/white spots in newborns. Resolves naturally.
- **Molluscum contagiosum:** Small, raised spots (skin-coloured/pink/brown). Usually treated at home.
- **Nappy rash:** Red, sore bottom. Usually treated at home.
- **Baby acne (neonatal acne):** Spots on face/scalp/chest. Resolves naturally.
- **Cradle cap:** Yellow/white scaly patches on scalp. Usually treated at home.

## Notes
- Most rashes are mild and self-limiting.
- Some rashes (scarlet fever, measles, impetigo) require GP care.
- Serious symptoms with rash require immediate emergency help.
