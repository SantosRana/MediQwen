# Cough

## Overview
- A cough usually clears up on its own within **3–4 weeks**.
- Most cases are mild and self-limiting.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Rest
- Drink plenty of fluids
- Stay at home if you have a high temperature or feel unwell
- Use paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain
- Try hot lemon and honey (not for babies <1 year)
- Herbal medicine *pelargonium* (for ages 12+)

**Note:** Limited evidence supports hot lemon, honey, or herbal remedies.  
Always check suitability of medicines (especially if pregnant).

## Pharmacist Help
Pharmacists can advise on:
- Cough syrup
- Cough medicine (not for children <12)
- Cough sweets

**Not effective:** Decongestants and codeine-based cough medicines.

## When to See a GP
See a GP if:
- Cough lasts >3 weeks (persistent cough)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Weakened immune system (e.g., chemotherapy, diabetes)

## Urgent GP / NHS 111
Seek urgent help if:
- Cough is very bad or worsening quickly (hacking cough, cannot stop)
- Feeling very unwell
- Chest pain
- Swollen, painful neck glands
- Difficulty breathing
- Coughing up blood

## GP Appointment
GP may:
- Listen to chest with stethoscope
- Take mucus sample
- Order X-ray, allergy test, or lung function test
- Rarely, refer to hospital specialist

**Important:** Antibiotics are not usually prescribed for coughs. Only used if bacterial infection or risk of complications.

## Causes
Most coughs are caused by:
- Cold or flu

Other causes:
- Smoking
- Heartburn (acid reflux)
- Allergies (e.g., hay fever)
- Infections (bronchitis, COVID-19)
- Post-nasal drip (mucus from nose/throat)

Rare cause:
- Lung cancer

## Notes
- Most coughs resolve naturally.
- Persistent or severe coughs require medical evaluation.
- Self-care and pharmacist advice are usually sufficient.
