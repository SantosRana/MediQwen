# Excessive Sweating (Hyperhidrosis)

## Overview
- Excessive sweating is common and can affect the whole body or specific areas.
- Sometimes improves with age.
- Can occur without obvious reason, due to another condition, or as a side effect of medication.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Wear loose-fitting clothes
- Wear moisture-absorbing socks, change at least twice daily
- Wear leather shoes and rotate footwear

**Don’t:**
- Avoid tight clothes or synthetic fabrics (e.g., nylon)
- Avoid enclosed boots or sports shoes
- Avoid triggers such as alcohol or spicy food

## Pharmacist Help
Pharmacists can recommend:
- Stronger antiperspirants (instead of deodorant)
- Armpit or sweat shields
- Foot powders for sweaty feet
- Soap substitutes gentle on skin

## When to See a GP
Seek medical advice if:
- Self-care measures do not help
- Sweating persists ≥6 months
- Sweating interferes with daily activities
- Occurs at least once a week
- Night sweats
- Family history of excessive sweating
- Sweating linked to medication use

GP may refer for tests if another condition is suspected.

## Specialist Treatments (Severe Cases)
If no obvious cause and self-care fails, dermatologist may recommend:
- Tablets to reduce sweating
- Iontophoresis (weak electric current through water/pad)
- Botox injections (for armpits; may not be NHS-covered)
- Surgery (e.g., sweat gland removal)

If sweating is secondary to another condition, treatment depends on the underlying cause.

## Notes
- Hyperhidrosis is manageable with lifestyle changes, pharmacy products, and medical treatments.
- Severity varies; most cases are mild but persistent sweating can impact quality of life.
