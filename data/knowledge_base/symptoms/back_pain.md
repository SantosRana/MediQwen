# Back Pain

## Overview
- Back pain, especially lower back pain, is very common.
- Usually improves within a few weeks but can recur or last longer.
- Self-care and lifestyle changes often help.

## Causes
- Injury (pulled muscle/strain)
- Slipped disc
- Sciatica (trapped nerve)
- Ankylosing spondylitis
- Rare causes: broken bone, cancer, infection

## When to See a GP
Seek medical advice if:
- Pain does not improve after a few weeks of home care
- Pain stops you from daily activities
- You’re worried or struggling to cope
- Unexplained weight loss
- Lump/swelling or change in back shape
- Pain worse at night or not relieved by rest
- Pain worsens with sneezing, coughing, or pooing
- Pain in upper back (between shoulders)

## Urgent GP / NHS 111
Seek urgent help if:
- Feeling hot, cold, shivery, or generally unwell
- Severe pain starting suddenly or worsening quickly

## Emergency (999 / A&E)
Call emergency services if:
- Pain, tingling, weakness, or numbness in both legs
- Loss of feeling around genitals or anus
- Bladder/bowel changes (difficulty peeing, incontinence)
- Sexual function changes (erection/orgasm issues, altered sensation)
- Chest pain
- Back pain after serious accident (e.g., car crash)

**Note:** Do not drive yourself to A&E. Bring current medicines with you.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Stay active, continue daily activities
- Take anti-inflammatory medicine (ibuprofen; paracetamol only with another painkiller)
- Check with pharmacist/GP if unsure about medicines
- Use ice pack (wrapped) to reduce pain/swelling
- Use heat pack (wrapped) for stiffness/muscle spasms
- Try exercises and stretches for back pain

**Don’t:**
- Do not stay in bed for long periods

## Exercises
- Gentle stretches and exercises can help recovery.
- Stop if pain worsens and seek GP advice.

## Notes
- Most back pain improves naturally.
- Staying active is key to recovery.
- Rarely, back pain signals serious conditions — always check if symptoms escalate.
