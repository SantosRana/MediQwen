# Nausea

## Overview
- Nausea is common and usually resolves on its own.
- Self-care measures often help relieve symptoms.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Get plenty of fresh air
- Distract yourself (music, film)
- Take regular sips of a cold drink
- Drink ginger or peppermint tea
- Eat ginger-containing foods (biscuits, etc.)
- Eat smaller, more frequent meals

**Don’t:**
- Avoid strong-smelling foods
- Avoid hot, fried, greasy foods
- Do not eat too quickly
- Avoid large drinks with meals
- Do not lie down soon after eating
- Avoid tight clothing around waist/tummy

## If You're Vomiting
- Vomiting can cause dehydration.
- Follow NHS guidance on managing vomiting.

## When to See a GP
See a GP if:
- Nausea persists for several days
- Nausea keeps coming back

GP may prescribe anti-sickness medicine if needed.  
Call **111** for advice if GP unavailable.

## Emergency (999 / A&E)
Call emergency services if nausea is sudden and accompanied by:
- Chest pain (tight/heavy)
- Pain spreading to arms, back, neck, jaw
- Shortness of breath  
These may indicate a **heart attack**.

## Causes
Nausea can result from many conditions. Examples:

| Other Symptoms | Possible Cause |
|----------------|----------------|
| Diarrhoea or vomiting | Norovirus, food poisoning |
| Headache + high temperature | Infection (e.g., flu) |
| Heartburn/bloating after eating | Acid reflux |
| Headache + light/sound sensitivity | Migraine |
| Dizziness | Labyrinthitis, vertigo |

Other causes:
- Pregnancy (morning sickness)
- Motion sickness
- Anxiety
- Alcohol
- Medicines
- Recent surgery

## Notes
- Nausea is usually mild and self-limiting.
- Try self-care first; seek GP advice if persistent.
- Do not self-diagnose — underlying causes vary.
