# Healthy Diets

## Key Facts
- Diet is a critical determinant of health and well-being.  
- Unhealthy diets are a major risk factor for disease and disability.  
- Healthy diets protect against malnutrition and noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) such as diabetes, heart disease, stroke, and cancer.  
- Breastfeeding fosters healthy growth and cognitive development.  
- Dietary behaviours established in childhood often persist into adulthood.  
- Four principles of healthy diets: **adequacy, balance, moderation, diversity**.  
- Diet must be safe, free from microbial and chemical contaminants.  
- Minimally processed foods low in unhealthy fats, free sugars, and sodium form the foundation.  

## Overview
- Healthy diets prevent malnutrition and NCDs across the life course.  
- Global dietary shifts: more processed foods high in fats, sugars, and sodium; insufficient fruit, vegetables, and fibre.  
- Diet composition varies by age, gender, lifestyle, culture, and local food availability.  
- Core principles remain universal: adequacy, balance, moderation, diversity.  

## WHO Guidance

### Carbohydrates
- 45–75% of daily energy from unrefined carbs (whole grains, pulses, fruits, vegetables).  
- ≥400 g fruits/vegetables daily for adults; adjusted lower for children.  
- ≥25 g dietary fibre daily for adults; adjusted lower for children.  
- Limit fruit juice due to free sugars.  

### Sugars
- Free sugars <10% of daily energy (≈50 g for 2000 kcal diet).  
- Further reduction to <5% provides additional benefits.  
- Free sugars include added sugars, honey, syrups, fruit juices.  
- Avoid non-sugar sweeteners (e.g., aspartame, sucralose, stevia).  

### Fats
- Adults: 15–30% of daily energy from fat.  
- Prefer unsaturated fats (fish, nuts, plant oils).  
- Limit saturated fat (<10% of energy) and trans fat (<1%).  
- Industrial trans fats should be eliminated.  
- Replace with polyunsaturated/monounsaturated fats or fibre-rich carbs.  

### Protein
- 10–15% of daily energy (≈50–75 g for 2000 kcal diet).  
- Higher intake acceptable for adolescents, athletes.  
- Mix of animal and plant sources; plant-based proteins beneficial for adults.  
- Animal sources important for children and pregnant/lactating women.  

### Salt & Potassium
- Adults: <5 g salt/day (<2 g sodium).  
- Potassium ≥3510 mg/day for adults.  
- Reduce salt from processed foods, condiments, snacks.  
- Increase potassium via fruits and vegetables.  
- Lower-sodium salt substitutes recommended where feasible.  

### Micronutrients
- 30 essential vitamins/minerals (e.g., iron, iodine, zinc, calcium, vitamins A, B, C, D, E, K).  
- Deficiencies common globally, especially iron, zinc, vitamin A, folate.  
- Encourage nutrient-dense foods (fruits, vegetables, legumes, lean meats).  
- Large-scale food fortification recommended in high-deficiency settings.  

## Foods
- Diverse diets across food groups reduce NCD risk.  
- Plant-based protein shift beneficial for adults.  
- Limit highly processed foods high in sodium, sugar, unhealthy fats.  

## Infants & Young Children
- Exclusive breastfeeding for first 6 months.  
- Continued breastfeeding until ≥2 years.  
- Complementary foods from 6 months: safe, nutrient-dense, no added salt/sugar.  
- Diverse diet including animal-source foods, fruits, vegetables.  
- Avoid starchy staples, sugary foods, trans fats.  

## Promoting Healthy Diets
- Influenced by income, food prices, preferences, culture, environment.  
- Governments play central role in creating healthy food environments.  
- Policy actions:  
  - Subsidize fruit/vegetable production.  
  - Tax/reduce incentives for unhealthy processed foods.  
  - Reformulate products to reduce fat, sugar, sodium.  
  - Protect children from harmful food marketing.  
  - Ensure healthy food availability in schools, workplaces, public institutions.  
  - Nutrition labelling and front-of-pack interpretive labels.  
  - Nutrition counselling in primary care.  
  - Promote breastfeeding through supportive policies.  

## Notes
- Healthy diets are foundational for lifelong health.  
- Require multisectoral collaboration (health, education, agriculture, trade, environment).  
- Policy coherence and consumer awareness are essential for sustainable change.
