# Sanitation & Health

## Key Facts
- In 2022, **57% of the global population (4.6 billion)** used safely managed sanitation.  
- **1.5 billion people** still lack basic sanitation; **419 million** practice open defecation.  
- In 2020, **44% of household wastewater** was discharged untreated.  
- At least **10% of the world’s population** consumes food irrigated by wastewater.  
- Poor sanitation linked to diarrhoeal diseases (cholera, dysentery, typhoid), intestinal worms, polio, stunting, and antimicrobial resistance.  

## Overview
- Inadequate water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) cause **1.4 million deaths annually**, mostly in low- and middle-income countries.  
- Unsafe sanitation alone accounts for **564,000 deaths**, largely from diarrhoea.  
- Diarrhoea remains a major killer of children under 5, but is preventable.  
- Open defecation perpetuates disease, poverty, malnutrition, and inequality.  

## Benefits of Improved Sanitation
- Reduces spread of intestinal worms, schistosomiasis, trachoma.  
- Decreases malnutrition severity.  
- Promotes dignity and safety, especially for women and girls.  
- Boosts school attendance (especially girls with separate facilities).  
- Reduces antimicrobial resistance.  
- Enables safe recovery of water, nutrients, and energy from wastewater/sludge.  
- Strengthens resilience to climate shocks (e.g., safe wastewater use for irrigation).  
- WHO study (2012): **US$1 invested → US$5.50 return** in health savings, productivity, fewer premature deaths.  

## Challenges
- UN call (2013): eliminate open defecation by 2025. Progress ongoing but needs acceleration.  
- Universal basic sanitation by 2030 requires doubling progress; safely managed services need five-fold increase.  
- Urban challenges: lack of sewerage, limited space, unsafe pits/tanks, unaffordable sludge removal.  
- Climate change (floods, droughts, sea level rise) threatens existing services.  
- Wastewater/sludge valuable in circular economy but often used unsafely.  
- UN-Water launched **SDG6 Global Acceleration Framework (2019)**; WHO/UNICEF issued **State of the World’s Sanitation Report (2020)**.  

## WHO Response
- UN recognized water and sanitation as a **human right (2010)**.  
- SDG 6.2: adequate, equitable sanitation for all.  
- SDG 6.3: halve untreated wastewater, increase safe reuse.  
- WHO leads global monitoring of sanitation burden (SDG 3.9), access (SDG 6.2, 6.3), and enabling factors (SDG 6a, 6b, GLAAS).  
- Provides data to guide investment in toilets, wastewater management, and excreta safety.  

## Notes
- Sanitation is central to health, dignity, and development.  
- Accelerated progress is essential to meet SDG targets and protect vulnerable populations.  
- Safe reuse of wastewater/sludge offers opportunities if managed properly.
