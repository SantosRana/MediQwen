# Family Planning & Contraception

## Key Facts
- In 2021, among 1.9 billion women of reproductive age (15–49), **1.1 billion had a need for family planning**.  
- Of these, **874 million used modern contraceptive methods**, while **164 million had unmet need**.  
- Contraception supports the human right to decide freely and responsibly the number and spacing of children.  
- Prevents unintended pregnancies and reduces related health risks.  
- Only **condoms** prevent both pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including HIV.  

## Overview
- Family planning enables people to achieve desired number and spacing of children.  
- Achieved through contraceptive methods and infertility treatment.  
- Supports sexual and reproductive health rights, promotes health, gender equality, education, and economic opportunity.  
- Directly aligns with **SDG 3.7** (universal access to sexual and reproductive health care).  

## Impact
- Reduces pregnancy-related health risks, especially for adolescents.  
- Enables women to pursue education and employment.  
- Strengthens health systems and promotes economic development.  
- By 2030, an additional **70 million women** projected to gain access to modern contraception.  

## Barriers
- Limited availability in low-resource/rural settings.  
- Barriers related to age, income, marital status.  
- Fear of side effects, misinformation, social/religious opposition.  
- Poor quality services, provider bias.  
- Gender inequality: partner consent requirements, stigma.  

## Contraceptive Methods
- **Highly effective, long-lasting:** implants, IUDs, sterilization.  
- **Regular use:** pills, injections, patches, vaginal rings.  
- **Barrier methods:** condoms (only method preventing both pregnancy + STIs).  
- **Less reliable:** fertility awareness-based methods, withdrawal.  
- Choice depends on health, preferences, needs; health worker advice recommended.  

## Infertility
- Defined as inability to conceive after 12 months of regular unprotected sex.  
- Affects ~1 in 6 adults during lifetime.  
- Causes: infections, medical conditions, environmental/lifestyle factors.  
- Both men and women affected; women often face stigma.  
- Treating infertility is part of comprehensive family planning.  
- Modern contraceptives **do not cause infertility**.  

## WHO Response
- Promotes universal access to sexual and reproductive health services.  
- Produces evidence-based guidelines on safety, service delivery, and human rights.  
- Assists countries in adapting and implementing tools.  
- Participates in developing new contraceptive technologies.  
- Conducts research to improve access to information and services.  

## Notes
- Family planning is central to health, rights, and development.  
- Scaling up access requires addressing barriers, ensuring equity, and expanding contraceptive choices.
