# Ebola Disease

## Overview
- Caused by viruses in the **Orthoebolavirus genus** (Filoviridae family).
- Six species identified; three cause large outbreaks:  
  - **EBOV** → Ebola virus disease (EVD).  
  - **SUDV** → Sudan virus disease (SVD).  
  - **BDBV** → Bundibugyo virus disease (BVD).  
- First outbreaks: 1976 in South Sudan (SVD) and DRC (EVD).
- Licensed vaccines and therapeutics exist for EVD, but not for SVD or BVD.
- Early supportive care (rehydration, symptom management) improves survival.

## Transmission
- **Animal-to-human:** contact with blood/fluids/organs of infected animals (fruit bats, primates, antelope, porcupines).  
- **Human-to-human:** direct contact with blood/fluids of infected or deceased patients; contaminated surfaces.  
- Not infectious before symptoms appear; infectious as long as blood contains virus.  
- High risk for health workers without strict infection control.  
- Burial practices involving contact with bodies increase transmission.

## Symptoms
- Incubation: 2–21 days.  
- Early: fever, fatigue, malaise, muscle pain, headache, sore throat.  
- Later: vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, rash, kidney/liver impairment.  
- Bleeding (internal/external) less common but possible: nose, gums, vagina, injection sites.  
- CNS effects: confusion, irritability, aggression.

## Diagnosis
- Difficult to distinguish clinically from malaria, typhoid, shigellosis, meningitis, other viral haemorrhagic fevers.  
- Confirmed via:  
  - RT-PCR assay.  
  - ELISA (antibody/antigen capture).  
  - Virus isolation (cell culture).  
- Samples are extreme biohazard; require maximum containment labs.

## Treatment
- **Optimized supportive care:** pain management, nutrition, co-infection treatment.  
- **EVD-specific therapeutics:** monoclonal antibodies (mAb114/Ansuvimab™, REGN-EB3/Inmazeb™).  
- No approved treatments yet for SVD or BVD; candidate products in development.

## Vaccines
- **EVD:**  
  - Ervebo (Merck).  
  - Zabdeno + Mvabea (Janssen).  
  - Used in outbreak response and preventive vaccination for health/frontline workers.  
- **SVD/BVD:** candidate vaccines under evaluation; CORE protocol available for trials.

## Prevention & Control
- Community engagement critical.  
- Outbreak control: clinical care, surveillance, contact tracing, lab services, infection prevention, safe burials, vaccination (EVD only), social mobilization.  
- Risk reduction: avoid wildlife contact/consumption, avoid close contact with infected individuals, isolate patients in treatment centres.  
- Outbreak containment: safe burials, contact monitoring (21 days), hygiene, environmental cleaning.

## Infection Control in Healthcare
- Standard precautions: hand hygiene, PPE, safe injection practices, safe burials.  
- Extra measures for suspected/confirmed cases: prevent contact with fluids, contaminated surfaces.  
- Laboratory staff: handle samples only in high-containment labs.

## Survivors
- Survivors should be respected and supported; no isolation needed once blood tests negative.  
- Possible sequelae: clinical, psychological, social.  
- Virus persistence in immune-privileged sites (testicles, eye, brain).  
- Rare relapse documented.  
- Ebola virus detected in semen up to 15 months post-recovery.  
- WHO recommends semen testing programmes and safer sex practices until two consecutive negative tests.

## Notes
- Ebola remains a high-risk infectious disease with potential for large outbreaks.  
- Vaccination and therapeutics exist only for EVD; supportive care essential for all.  
- Community involvement and strict infection control are key to containment.
