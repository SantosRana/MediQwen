# Gonorrhoea (Neisseria gonorrhoeae Infection)

## Overview
- Common sexually transmitted infection (STI) caused by *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*.
- Spread via vaginal, oral, or anal sex.
- Treatable and curable with antibiotics, but resistance is a growing threat.
- Condom use prevents most cases.
- Can be passed from mother to baby during childbirth.
- Gonococcal infection increases risk of HIV transmission.

## Scope of the Problem
- WHO estimated 82.4 million new infections in 2020 among adults aged 15–49.
- Highest prevalence in priority populations: men who have sex with men, sex workers, transgender women, adolescents, and young people in high-burden countries.
- Rising antimicrobial resistance threatens treatment effectiveness.

## Signs & Symptoms
- **Onset:** 1–14 days after sexual contact.
- **Men:** penile discharge (white/yellow/green), painful urination, swollen testes.  
- **Women:** often asymptomatic; possible vaginal discharge, painful urination, bleeding between periods or during sex.  
- **Anal infection:** discharge, bleeding, itchiness, soreness, painful bowel movements.  
- **Throat infection:** usually asymptomatic; possible sore throat, redness, pain.  
- **Infants:** eye infection (redness, pain, ulcers, tearing), preventable with eye medication.

## Complications
- **Women:** pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), ectopic pregnancy, infertility.  
- **Men:** scrotal swelling, urethral stricture, infertility.  
- **Infants:** untreated eye infection → blindness.  
- **Rare:** disseminated gonococcal infection (fever, skin, heart, joints, meninges).  
- Social stigma and relationship impact.

## Diagnosis
- **Gold standard:** molecular tests (lab or point-of-care).  
- Other methods: ELISA, Gram stain microscopy (less sensitive in women/throat/anal infections).  
- Syndromic approach used in low-resource settings.  
- Samples: urine (men), swabs (women, anus, throat, conjunctiva).  
- Screening recommended for high-risk populations.  
- Antimicrobial sensitivity testing essential in treatment failure cases.

## Treatment
- **Preferred:** ceftriaxone (injection).  
- **Alternative:** cefixime (oral), sometimes combined with azithromycin if ceftriaxone not feasible.  
- Avoid sex for 7 days after treatment; notify partners for testing/treatment.  
- Treatment failure causes: non-adherence, reinfection, resistance, co-infections.  
- Complete treatment until cured.

## Prevention
- Consistent condom use.  
- Partner notification and testing.  
- Antibiotic eye ointment for newborns.  
- No specific vaccine yet; meningococcal type B vaccine (4CMenB) shows cross-protection potential. WHO monitoring trials.

## Notes
- Gonorrhoea remains a major global STI threat due to rising resistance.  
- Early diagnosis, rational antibiotic use, and partner treatment are key to control.
