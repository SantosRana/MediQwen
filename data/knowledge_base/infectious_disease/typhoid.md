# Typhoid Fever

## Overview
- Life-threatening infection caused by *Salmonella Typhi*.  
- Spread through contaminated food or water.  
- Bacteria multiply in the intestine and spread into the bloodstream.  
- Urbanization, climate change, and antimicrobial resistance increase global burden.  
- Preventable with safe water, sanitation, hygiene, and vaccination.  

## Symptoms
- *S. Typhi* lives only in humans.  
- Carried in bloodstream and intestinal tract.  
- Symptoms: prolonged high fever, fatigue, headache, nausea, abdominal pain, constipation or diarrhoea, sometimes rash.  
- Severe cases → complications or death.  
- Confirmed via blood testing.  

## Epidemiology & Burden
- Improved living conditions and antibiotics reduced typhoid in industrialized countries.  
- Still a major public health problem in Africa, Eastern Mediterranean, South-East Asia, Western Pacific.  
- 2019 estimates: ~9 million cases annually, ~110,000 deaths.  
- Highest risk: populations lacking safe water/sanitation; children most vulnerable.  

## Treatment
- Treated with antibiotics.  
- Antimicrobial resistance common, complicating treatment.  
- Even after symptoms resolve, carriers may shed bacteria in faeces.  
- Patients should:  
  - Complete prescribed antibiotics.  
  - Wash hands after bathroom use.  
  - Avoid preparing food for others.  
  - Get tested to confirm clearance of bacteria.  

## Prevention
- Safe water, sanitation, hygiene among food handlers, and vaccination.  
- **Typhoid conjugate vaccine (TCV):**  
  - Purified Vi antigen linked to carrier protein.  
  - Single injectable dose for children ≥6 months and adults (up to 45–65 years depending on vaccine).  
- **Other vaccines:**  
  - Injectable purified antigen (≥2 years old).  
  - Live attenuated oral capsule (≥6 years old).  
  - Provide shorter immunity; not for children <2 years.  
- WHO prequalified two TCVs since 2017; introduced into childhood immunization in endemic countries.  

## Travellers
- Risk higher in endemic areas, lower in tourist/business centres with good sanitation.  
- Vaccination recommended for travellers to high-risk destinations.  
- Safety tips:  
  - Eat properly cooked, hot food.  
  - Avoid raw milk; drink pasteurized/boiled milk.  
  - Avoid ice unless made from safe water.  
  - Boil/disinfect questionable water.  
  - Wash hands thoroughly, especially after toilet use or animal contact.  
  - Wash/peel fruits and vegetables before eating raw.  

## Notes
- Typhoid remains a major global health challenge due to poor sanitation and rising drug resistance.  
- Vaccination, hygiene, and safe water access are critical to prevention.
