# Measles

## Overview
- Highly contagious airborne viral disease.  
- Can cause severe complications and death.  
- Vaccination prevented ~59 million deaths between 2000–2024.  
- Despite safe, cost-effective vaccine, ~95,000 deaths occurred in 2024, mostly in unvaccinated children <5 years.  
- Before vaccines (1963), epidemics every 2–3 years caused ~2.6 million deaths annually.  

## Signs & Symptoms
- Onset: 10–14 days after exposure.  
- **Early (4–7 days):** runny nose, cough, red watery eyes, small white spots inside cheeks.  
- **Rash:** begins 7–18 days after exposure, starts on face/neck, spreads to hands/feet over 3 days, lasts 5–6 days.  
- **Complications:** blindness, encephalitis, severe diarrhoea/dehydration, ear infections, pneumonia.  
- Dangerous in pregnancy (premature, low birth weight).  
- Higher risk in malnourished children, vitamin A deficiency, HIV-positive individuals.  
- Measles weakens immune system, increasing vulnerability to other infections.  

## Who is at Risk
- Any non-immune person (unvaccinated or failed immunity).  
- Highest risk: unvaccinated children, pregnant women.  
- Common in Africa, Middle East, Asia.  
- Deaths concentrated in low-income countries with weak health systems.  
- Risk increased in conflict zones, disaster recovery, refugee camps.  

## Transmission
- One of the most contagious diseases (R0 up to 18).  
- Spread via nasal/throat secretions (coughing, sneezing) or airborne particles.  
- Virus remains active in air/surfaces for up to 2 hours.  
- Infectious from 4 days before rash onset to 4 days after.  
- Imported cases remain a threat in near-elimination countries.  

## Treatment
- No specific antiviral treatment.  
- Supportive care: hydration, nutrition, symptom relief.  
- Antibiotics for pneumonia, ear/eye infections.  
- **Vitamin A supplements:** two doses 24 hours apart for all measles patients; prevents blindness, reduces mortality.  

## Prevention
- **Vaccination:**  
  - Safe, effective, inexpensive (<US$1 per child).  
  - Two doses required for immunity.  
  - First dose: 9 months (high-risk countries) or 12–15 months.  
  - Second dose: 15–18 months.  
  - Often combined with mumps, rubella, varicella vaccines.  
- Routine vaccination + mass campaigns reduce deaths.  
- Used in emergencies to stop outbreaks, especially among refugees.  
- Combination vaccines reduce delivery costs and add rubella protection.  

## Notes
- Measles remains a major cause of child mortality despite vaccine availability.  
- Global immunization coverage (first dose) was 84% in 2024, below 2019 levels (86%).  
- Strengthening vaccination campaigns is critical to prevent resurgence.
