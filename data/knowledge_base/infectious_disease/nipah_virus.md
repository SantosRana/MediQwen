# Nipah Virus Infection

## Overview
- **Zoonotic virus** transmitted from animals to humans, contaminated food, or directly between people.  
- First identified in 1998 in Malaysia among pig farmers; outbreaks later in Singapore, Bangladesh, India, and the Philippines.  
- Ongoing outbreaks in Bangladesh (almost yearly) and periodic outbreaks in India, including 2026.  
- Natural host: fruit bats (*Pteropodidae* family).  
- Causes severe disease with high fatality rates.  
- No approved drugs or vaccines; supportive care is critical.  

## Transmission
- **Animal-to-human:** contact with bats, pigs, horses; consumption of contaminated fruit/date palm sap.  
- **Human-to-human:** close contact in households, health-care settings, caregivers.  
- Risk higher in overcrowded, poorly ventilated hospitals without infection control.  
- **Other routes:** contaminated food, needle injuries, blood transfusion (rare).  
- Fruit bats in Asia and Africa carry antibodies, suggesting wider distribution.  

## Signs & Symptoms
- Incubation: 3–14 days (rarely up to 45).  
- Some asymptomatic; most develop fever, headache, confusion, cough, difficulty breathing.  
- Other symptoms: chills, fatigue, dizziness, vomiting, diarrhoea.  
- Severe disease: encephalitis (brain swelling), pneumonia, multi-organ involvement, death.  
- Long-term neurological sequelae in ~20% of survivors.  

## Diagnosis
- Difficult to distinguish clinically from other encephalitis/pneumonia.  
- **Preferred test:** RT-PCR of respiratory samples, blood, cerebrospinal fluid.  
- ELISA antibody detection also used.  
- Samples are biohazard; require maximum containment labs.  
- Must be handled by trained staff in equipped facilities.  

## Treatment
- No specific antiviral treatment.  
- Supportive care essential:  
  - Manage complications (encephalitis, pneumonia, organ damage).  
  - Oxygen therapy, ventilation, renal dialysis if needed.  
  - Rehydration, nutrition, close monitoring.  
- Candidate drugs and vaccines under development; WHO prioritizes Nipah in R&D Blueprint.  

## Prevention
**Bat-to-human:**  
- Cover date palm sap collection sites.  
- Boil juice, wash/peel fruit, discard fruit with bat bites.  

**Animal-to-human:**  
- Wear protective clothing when handling sick animals.  
- Protect pig feed/sheds from bats.  
- Farm measures: cleaning/disinfection, quarantine, culling, restricting animal movement.  

**Human-to-human:**  
- Early referral to health facilities.  
- Avoid close unprotected contact with sick people.  
- Hand hygiene after caring for patients.  

**Healthcare settings:**  
- Standard precautions for all patients.  
- Suspected/confirmed cases: single-patient rooms.  
- Contact/droplet precautions: mask, eye protection, gown, gloves.  
- Airborne precautions for aerosol-generating procedures (isolation rooms, respirators).  
- Family/caregivers should follow same precautions.  

## Notes
- Nipah virus remains a high-risk emerging infection with potential for severe outbreaks.  
- Prevention relies on awareness, infection control, and reducing bat/animal exposure.  
- Continued research into vaccines and therapeutics is essential.
