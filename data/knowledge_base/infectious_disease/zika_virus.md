# Zika Virus Infection

## Overview
- Mosquito-borne virus first identified in Uganda (1947).  
- Sporadic infections across Africa/Asia until 2007; major outbreaks since in Americas, Asia, Pacific.  
- Associated with **Guillain-Barré syndrome** and **congenital malformations** (microcephaly, congenital Zika syndrome).  
- WHO declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern in 2016.  
- Transmission persists at low levels in several endemic regions; 92 countries/territories reported cases.  

## Symptoms
- Most infections asymptomatic.  
- Onset: 3–14 days after infection.  
- Mild symptoms: rash, fever, conjunctivitis, muscle/joint pain, malaise, headache.  
- Duration: 2–7 days.  
- Laboratory confirmation required (symptoms overlap with other arboviruses).  

## Complications
- **Pregnancy:** congenital Zika syndrome (microcephaly, limb contractures, high muscle tone, eye/ear abnormalities, hearing loss).  
- Risk estimated at 5–15% of infants born to infected mothers.  
- Other pregnancy outcomes: fetal loss, stillbirth, preterm birth.  
- **Neurological:** Guillain-Barré syndrome, neuropathy, myelitis.  
- Long-term effects under investigation.  

## Transmission
- Primarily via **Aedes aegypti** and **Aedes albopictus** mosquitoes.  
- Other routes: mother-to-fetus, sexual contact, lab exposure, blood transfusion, possibly organ transplantation.  
- Mosquitoes bite mainly during the day.  

## Diagnosis
- Suspected based on symptoms + exposure history.  
- Confirmed via laboratory tests:  
  - RT-PCR detection of viral RNA.  
  - IgM antibody testing (must differentiate from dengue and other flaviviruses).  

## Treatment
- No specific antiviral treatment.  
- Supportive care: rest, fluids, antipyretics/analgesics.  
- If dengue cannot be ruled out, avoid NSAIDs (bleeding risk).  
- Pregnant women in endemic areas should seek testing and counselling.  

## Prevention
- **No vaccine available yet**; research ongoing.  
- **Mosquito bite prevention:**  
  - Wear light-coloured, covering clothing.  
  - Use window screens, closed doors/windows.  
  - Apply repellents (DEET, IR3535, icaridin).  
  - Sleep under mosquito nets (especially pregnant women, children).  
  - Eliminate breeding sites (water containers, flowerpots, tires).  
  - Community initiatives + larvicides/insecticides recommended.  
- **Sexual transmission prevention:**  
  - Safer sex or abstinence during pregnancy.  
  - Men returning from endemic areas: abstain/use condoms for 3 months.  
  - Women returning: abstain/use condoms for 2 months.  
  - Partners of pregnant women in endemic areas: safer sex/abstinence throughout pregnancy.  

## Notes
- Zika remains a global health concern despite declining cases since 2017.  
- Prevention focuses on mosquito control, safe sex practices, and protecting pregnant women.  
- Research continues on vaccines and long-term neurological impacts.
