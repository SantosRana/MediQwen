# Mpox (Monkeypox Virus Disease)

## Overview
- Infectious disease caused by **monkeypox virus (MPXV)**, an Orthopoxvirus in the Poxviridae family.  
- Two clades: **Clade I (Ia, Ib)** and **Clade II (IIa, IIb)**.  
- Global outbreak of clade IIb began in 2022; clade Ib spreading beyond Africa since 2024.  
- Natural reservoir unknown; small mammals (squirrels, monkeys) susceptible.  
- Most recover fully, but some develop severe illness or complications.  

## Transmission
- **Human-to-human:** close contact (skin-to-skin, sex, kissing, face-to-face respiratory exposure).  
- Contaminated objects (clothing, linen), needle injuries, tattoo parlours.  
- **Mother-to-child:** during pregnancy or birth; can cause miscarriage, stillbirth, neonatal death.  
- **Animal-to-human:** bites, scratches, hunting, skinning, cooking, eating infected animals.  
- Infectious until all sores heal and new skin forms.  
- Asymptomatic transmission reported but uncommon.  

## Signs & Symptoms
- Onset: 1–21 days after exposure; lasts 2–4 weeks.  
- **Common:** rash, fever, sore throat, headache, muscle aches, back pain, low energy, swollen lymph nodes.  
- Rash: starts as flat sore → blister → crust → heals. Can appear on face, palms, soles, genitals, anus, mouth, throat.  
- Complications: bacterial skin infections, pneumonia, corneal infection (vision loss), encephalitis, myocarditis, sepsis, proctitis, balanitis, urethritis.  
- Higher risk of severe illness: children, pregnant people, immunocompromised (e.g., uncontrolled HIV).  

## Diagnosis
- Difficult to distinguish from chickenpox, measles, scabies, herpes, syphilis, STIs, allergies.  
- Preferred test: **PCR detection of viral DNA** from rash swabs (skin, fluid, crusts).  
- Alternative: throat or anal swabs if no lesions.  
- Blood testing not recommended.  
- HIV testing advised for adults with mpox; STI testing considered.  

## Treatment & Vaccination
- Supportive care: rash management, pain relief, prevent complications.  
- **Vaccination:**  
  - Pre-exposure prophylaxis for high-risk groups (health workers, household contacts, MSM, sex workers).  
  - Post-exposure prophylaxis: vaccine within 4 days of contact (up to 14 days if asymptomatic).  
- No proven antivirals; some under emergency use authorization and clinical trials.  
- ART should continue for HIV-positive individuals; initiate within 7 days of HIV diagnosis.  

## Self-care & Prevention
**Do:**  
- Isolate at home or hospital until lesions heal.  
- Wash hands often; wear mask; cover lesions.  
- Keep skin dry/uncovered unless around others.  
- Disinfect shared spaces.  
- Saltwater rinses for mouth sores; warm baths with baking soda/Epsom salts.  
- Use paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain.  

**Don’t:**  
- Pop blisters or scratch sores.  
- Shave affected areas until healed.  

**Precautions:**  
- Use condoms for 12 weeks after recovery (precautionary).  
- Avoid new sexual partners during outbreaks.  
- Monitor for symptoms for 21 days after exposure.  

## Notes
- Mpox remains a global health concern with multiple clades circulating.  
- Supportive care, vaccination, and community awareness are key to reducing spread and complications.
