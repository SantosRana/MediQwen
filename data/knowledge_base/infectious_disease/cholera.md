# Cholera

## Key Facts
- Severe diarrheal disease, can be fatal within hours if untreated.
- Estimated 1.3–4.0 million cases and 21,000–143,000 deaths annually worldwide.
- Most cases mild; treated with oral rehydration solution (ORS).
- Severe cases require intravenous fluids, ORS, and antibiotics.
- Safe water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) are essential for prevention.
- Oral cholera vaccine (OCV) helps prevent and control outbreaks.

## Overview
- Acute diarrheal infection caused by *Vibrio cholerae* in contaminated food/water.
- Global public health threat, linked to inequity and poor development.
- Rapid treatment is vital to save lives.
- Strong surveillance systems needed for outbreak detection and response.

## Symptoms
- Severe acute watery diarrhoea, fatal within hours if untreated.
- Most infected show no symptoms but can spread bacteria via faeces for 1–10 days.
- Symptoms appear 12 hours–5 days after infection.
- Severe cases: life-threatening dehydration.

## History
- Known for centuries; first pandemic in 19th century.
- Six pandemics killed millions globally.
- Current (seventh) pandemic began in South Asia in 1961 and continues.

## Strains
- Only *V. cholerae* O1 and O139 cause outbreaks.
- O1 responsible for recent outbreaks; O139 now rare.
- Illness caused by both strains is similar.

## Epidemiology & Burden
- Outbreaks linked to poor WASH access, conflict, displacement, climate events.
- Rising cases: 535,321 cases and 4,007 deaths reported in 2023 across 45 countries.
- True burden likely higher due to underreporting and weak surveillance.

## Prevention & Control
- Strengthen surveillance, improve WASH, risk communication, treatment access, OCV campaigns.
- Integrated surveillance with rapid diagnostic tests (RDTs) and lab confirmation (culture, PCR).
- WASH interventions: safe drinking water, sanitation, hygiene promotion, water quality monitoring, healthcare facility improvements.

## Treatment
- Easily treatable with prompt ORS.
- Severe dehydration: rapid IV fluids, ORS, antibiotics.
- Case fatality rate <1% in treatment centres.
- Community access to ORS essential during outbreaks.
- Mass antibiotic prophylaxis not recommended (no effect, risk of resistance).

## Notes
- Long-term control depends on economic development and universal WASH access.
- Cholera prevention aligns with Sustainable Development Goals.
