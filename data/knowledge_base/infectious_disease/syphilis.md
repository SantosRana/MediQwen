# Syphilis

## Overview
- Preventable and curable bacterial STI caused by *Treponema pallidum*.  
- Transmitted via oral, vaginal, anal sex, pregnancy, and rarely blood transfusion.  
- Untreated syphilis can last years and cause severe complications.  
- Congenital syphilis leads to stillbirth, neonatal death, or lifelong health problems.  
- Condom use prevents most cases.  
- Rapid tests allow same-day diagnosis and treatment.  

## Symptoms
- Many people asymptomatic or unnoticed.  
- **Primary syphilis:**  
  - Lasts ~21 days.  
  - Painless chancre sore on genitals, anus, or elsewhere.  
  - Heals in 3–10 days but remains infectious.  
- **Secondary syphilis:**  
  - Non-itchy rash (palms, soles).  
  - White/grey lesions in moist areas (labia, anus).  
  - Symptoms resolve without treatment.  
- **Latent syphilis:**  
  - No symptoms.  
  - Progresses to tertiary stage if untreated.  
- **Tertiary syphilis:**  
  - Brain, cardiovascular, and systemic disease.  
- **Congenital syphilis:**  
  - Rashes, organ inflammation, anaemia, bone/joint problems, neurological issues (blindness, deafness, meningitis), developmental delays, seizures.  

## Prevention
- Consistent condom use.  
- Annual testing for high-risk groups.  
- Pregnant women tested at first prenatal visit; immediate treatment prevents congenital syphilis.  
- Partner notification essential.  

## Diagnosis
- Based on clinical/sexual history, physical exam, lab tests.  
- Direct detection of *T. pallidum* via microscopy.  
- Indirect blood tests and rapid tests available.  
- Asymptomatic infections detected via screening.  
- No specific diagnostic test for congenital syphilis; infants of infected mothers must be examined and tested.  

## Treatment
- **First-line:** benzathine penicillin (BPG) injection.  
  - Only WHO-recommended treatment for pregnant women.  
  - Used for all stages (more doses for later stages).  
- **Second-line:** doxycycline, ceftriaxone, azithromycin.  
- BPG prevents mother-to-child transmission.  
- Babies born with syphilis require immediate treatment.  

## Transmission
- Sexual contact with infectious lesions (oral, anal, vaginal).  
- Vertical transmission during pregnancy.  
- Transmission most likely during early stages (within 2 years of infection).  

## Complications
- **HIV risk:** syphilis increases HIV acquisition risk ~2-fold.  
- **Tertiary syphilis:** decades later, affects brain, nerves, eyes, liver, heart, vessels, bones, joints; can cause death.  
- **Neurosyphilis:** headaches, muscle problems, dementia.  
- **Ocular syphilis:** eye pain, blurred vision, light sensitivity, blindness.  
- **Otosyphilis:** hearing/balance problems.  
- **Congenital syphilis:** stillbirth, neonatal death, prematurity, lifelong health issues.  

## Notes
- Syphilis remains a major global STI threat.  
- Early detection and treatment prevent severe complications and congenital transmission.
