# Seasonal Influenza (Flu)

## Overview
- Acute respiratory infection caused by influenza viruses.  
- Common worldwide; most people recover without treatment.  
- Spreads easily via coughing, sneezing, and contaminated hands.  
- Vaccination is the best preventive measure.  
- Four types: A, B, C, D.  
  - **A & B:** cause seasonal epidemics.  
  - **A:** subtypes (H1N1, H3N2); only type A causes pandemics.  
  - **B:** lineages (Yamagata, Victoria).  
  - **C:** mild infections, not significant for public health.  
  - **D:** affects cattle, not humans.  

## Signs & Symptoms
- Incubation: 1–4 days (average 2).  
- Sudden onset of fever, dry cough, headache, muscle/joint pain, malaise, sore throat, runny nose.  
- Cough may last 2+ weeks.  
- Most recover in ~1 week, but severe illness/death possible in high-risk groups.  
- Complications: pneumonia, sepsis, worsening of chronic diseases.  

## Epidemiology
- All age groups affected; higher risk for:  
  - Pregnant women.  
  - Children <5 years.  
  - Adults ≥65 years.  
  - People with chronic conditions (cardiac, pulmonary, renal, metabolic, neurodevelopmental, liver, hematologic).  
  - Immunosuppressed individuals (HIV, chemotherapy, malignancy).  
- Health workers at high risk due to exposure.  
- Causes absenteeism, productivity loss, and healthcare system strain.  
- 99% of influenza-related deaths in children <5 occur in developing countries.  

## Transmission
- Rapid spread in crowded areas (schools, nursing homes).  
- Droplet transmission via cough/sneeze; contaminated hands also spread virus.  
- Seasonal epidemics: winter in temperate climates; year-round outbreaks in tropics.  

## Diagnosis
- Clinical diagnosis common.  
- Lab confirmation: antigen detection, virus isolation, RT-PCR.  
- Rapid diagnostic tests available but less sensitive than RT-PCR.  

## Treatment
- Most recover without medical care.  
- Mild cases: rest, fluids, fever management, stay home to avoid spread.  
- Severe/high-risk cases: antiviral medications (early treatment critical).  
- WHO GISRS monitors antiviral resistance globally.  

## Prevention
- **Vaccination:**  
  - Safe, effective, used for >60 years.  
  - Annual vaccination recommended (immunity wanes).  
  - Especially important for high-risk groups and health workers.  
- **Other measures:**  
  - Handwashing.  
  - Cover mouth/nose when coughing/sneezing.  
  - Dispose of tissues properly.  
  - Stay home when unwell.  
  - Avoid close contact with sick people.  
  - Avoid touching eyes/nose/mouth.  

## Vaccines
- Updated annually to match circulating strains.  
- Types: inactivated (injectable), recombinant (injectable), live attenuated (nasal spray).  
- Less effective in older adults but reduces severity and complications.  

## Notes
- Seasonal influenza remains a major global health burden.  
- Vaccination and hygiene practices are key to prevention.  
- Surveillance systems guide vaccine composition and antiviral use.
