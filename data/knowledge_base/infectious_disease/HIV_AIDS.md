# HIV and AIDS

## Overview
- **HIV**: virus that attacks immune system, targeting white blood cells.  
- **AIDS**: most advanced stage of HIV infection.  
- Weakens immunity, increasing risk of TB, infections, and cancers.  
- Spread via blood, semen, vaginal fluids, breast milk.  
- Not spread by casual contact (kissing, hugging, sharing food).  
- Preventable and treatable with **antiretroviral therapy (ART)**.  
- Untreated HIV progresses to AIDS over years.  
- WHO defines **Advanced HIV Disease (AHD)**:  
  - CD4 <200 cells/mm³ or WHO stage 3/4 event in adults/adolescents.  
  - All children <5 years with HIV considered advanced regardless of status.  

## Signs & Symptoms
- **Early infection:** often asymptomatic; may have flu-like illness (fever, headache, rash, sore throat).  
- **Progression:** swollen lymph nodes, weight loss, fever, diarrhoea, cough.  
- **Severe illness (without treatment):** TB, cryptococcal meningitis, severe bacterial infections, cancers (lymphomas, Kaposi’s sarcoma).  
- HIV worsens other infections (hepatitis B/C, mpox).  

## Transmission
- Via body fluids: blood, semen, vaginal secretions, breast milk.  
- Mother-to-child during pregnancy, delivery, or breastfeeding.  
- Not transmitted through casual contact.  
- People on ART with undetectable viral load **do not transmit HIV**.  

## Risk Factors
- Unprotected anal/vaginal sex.  
- Other STIs (syphilis, herpes, chlamydia, gonorrhoea, bacterial vaginosis).  
- Harmful alcohol/drug use during sex.  
- Sharing contaminated needles/syringes.  
- Unsafe injections, transfusions, transplants.  
- Needle-stick injuries (health workers).  

## Diagnosis
- Rapid diagnostic tests (same-day results).  
- HIV self-tests available; confirmatory testing required.  
- Antibody tests detect infection after ~28 days (window period).  
- Retesting required before treatment enrolment.  
- Infants <18 months: need virological testing (not antibody tests).  
- New point-of-care technologies enable same-day infant diagnosis.  

## Prevention
- Consistent condom use.  
- HIV/STI testing.  
- Male circumcision.  
- Harm reduction services for drug users.  
- **PrEP (pre-exposure prophylaxis):**  
  - Oral tenofovir (TDF).  
  - Dapivirine vaginal ring.  
  - Long-acting injectable cabotegravir.  
  - Long-acting injectable lenacapavir.  
- **PEP (post-exposure prophylaxis):** ARVs after high-risk exposure.  
- ART prevents mother-to-child transmission.  
- People on ART with undetectable viral load do not transmit HIV.  

## Treatment
- No cure, but **ART** suppresses virus replication.  
- ART strengthens immune system, prevents opportunistic infections.  
- Must be taken daily for life.  
- ART allows full, healthy lives and prevents transmission.  
- Pregnant women should start ART early to protect mother and baby.  
- WHO supports **AHD package of care** to reduce illness/death.  
- Newer medicines and long-acting injectables (e.g., lenacapavir) are emerging.  

## Notes
- HIV remains a major global health challenge.  
- Early testing, ART access, and prevention strategies are critical.  
- Long-acting treatments may transform HIV care in the future.
