# Rabies

## Overview
- Vaccine-preventable, zoonotic viral disease affecting the central nervous system.  
- Dogs responsible for ~99% of human rabies cases.  
- Children aged 5–14 are frequent victims.  
- Present on all continents except Antarctica.  
- Estimated 59,000 deaths annually, mostly underreported.  
- Global cost: ~US$ 8.6 billion/year.  
- Once symptoms appear, rabies is virtually 100% fatal.  

## Transmission
- Spread via saliva through bites, scratches, or mucosal contact (eyes, mouth, wounds).  
- Infects mammals: dogs, cats, livestock, wildlife.  
- **Dog-mediated rabies:** primary source globally.  
- **Other sources:**  
  - Americas: hematophagous bats.  
  - Rare: foxes, raccoons, skunks.  
  - Rodents: not known to transmit rabies.  
  - Extremely rare: aerosols, raw meat/milk, organ transplantation.  
- Human-to-human transmission theoretically possible but never confirmed.  

## Symptoms
- Incubation: 2–3 months (range: 1 week–1 year).  
- Early: fever, pain, tingling/burning at wound site.  
- Progression: fatal brain/spinal cord inflammation.  
- **Forms:**  
  - **Furious rabies:** hyperactivity, hallucinations, hydrophobia, aerophobia; death in days.  
  - **Paralytic rabies:** gradual paralysis, coma, death; often misdiagnosed.  

## Diagnosis
- No WHO-approved tools for early detection before symptoms.  
- Clinical diagnosis difficult without exposure history or hydrophobia/aerophobia.  
- Postmortem confirmation: virus/antigen/nucleic acid detection in tissues (brain, skin, saliva).  
- Risk assessment critical for deciding on post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP).  

## Prevention
### Vaccinating Dogs
- Mass dog vaccination programs are most cost-effective prevention strategy.  
- Culling free-roaming dogs is ineffective.  

### Awareness
- Public education on dog behaviour, bite prevention, and responsible pet ownership.  

### Vaccinating People
- **Human vaccines:** WHO-prequalified (2024):  
  - RABIVAX-S (Serum Institute of India).  
  - VaxiRab N (Zydus Lifesciences).  
  - VERORAB (Sanofi Pasteur).  
- **Pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP):** for high-risk occupations (lab workers, animal handlers, wildlife rangers), travellers to endemic areas, people in remote endemic regions.  
- **Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP):**  
  - Immediate wound washing (≥15 minutes with soap/water).  
  - Rabies vaccine course.  
  - Rabies immunoglobulin or monoclonal antibodies (if indicated).  

### Exposure Categories
| Category | Description | PEP Measures |
|----------|-------------|--------------|
| I | Touching/feeding animals, licks on intact skin | Wash exposed skin, no PEP |
| II | Nibbling uncovered skin, minor scratches/abrasions without bleeding | Wound washing + immediate vaccination |
| III | Transdermal bites/scratches, mucous membrane exposure, bat contact | Wound washing + immediate vaccination + rabies immunoglobulin/monoclonal antibodies |

## Notes
- Rabies is a neglected tropical disease (NTD), disproportionately affecting marginalized populations.  
- PEP costs (~US$ 108) are unaffordable for many in low-income settings.  
- Over 29 million people receive rabies vaccine annually.  
- Comprehensive eradication requires dog vaccination, public awareness, and accessible human vaccines.
