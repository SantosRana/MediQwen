# Malaria

## Overview
- Life-threatening disease caused by *Plasmodium* parasites.  
- Spread by bites of infected female *Anopheles* mosquitoes.  
- Preventable and curable.  
- Not spread person-to-person.  
- Blood transfusion and contaminated needles can also transmit malaria.  
- **High-risk groups:** infants, children <5, pregnant women, travellers, people with HIV/AIDS.  

## Parasite Species
- Five species infect humans:  
  - **P. falciparum:** deadliest, most prevalent in Africa.  
  - **P. vivax:** dominant outside sub-Saharan Africa.  
  - **P. malariae, P. ovale, P. knowlesi:** less common.  

## Symptoms
- Onset: 10–15 days after mosquito bite.  
- **Mild:** fever, headache, chills.  
- **Severe:** fatigue, impaired consciousness, seizures, difficulty breathing, dark/bloody urine, jaundice, abnormal bleeding.  
- Severe malaria can cause death within 24 hours if untreated.  
- Malaria in pregnancy: premature delivery, low birth weight.  

## Disease Burden
- 2024: 282 million cases, 610,000 deaths (↑ from 2023).  
- WHO African Region: ~95% of cases/deaths.  
- Children <5: ~76% of deaths.  
- Nigeria, DRC, Niger account for ~50% of deaths.  

## Prevention
- Avoid mosquito bites:  
  - Mosquito nets.  
  - Repellents (DEET, IR3535, Icaridin).  
  - Coils, vaporizers.  
  - Protective clothing.  
  - Window screens.  
- **Chemoprophylaxis:** travellers should consult doctors before visiting endemic areas; start drugs 2–3 weeks before travel, continue 4 weeks after leaving risk area.  
- **Preventive chemotherapies:** PMC, SMC, IPTp, IPTsc, PDMC, MDA — targeted drug administration for vulnerable groups.  

## Vector Control
- Core interventions: insecticide-treated nets (ITNs), indoor residual spraying (IRS).  
- Emerging challenges: insecticide resistance, invasive *Anopheles stephensi* (urban, heat-tolerant, resistant).  
- New-generation nets provide improved protection.  

## Vaccines
- **RTS,S/AS01** (since 2021): recommended for children in moderate/high transmission areas.  
- **R21/Matrix-M** (since 2023): second approved vaccine.  
- Vaccines now integrated into routine childhood immunization in Africa.  
- Greatest impact when combined with other interventions (nets, chemoprophylaxis).  

## Treatment
- Early diagnosis and treatment essential.  
- Confirm suspected cases with microscopy or rapid diagnostic tests.  
- **First-line:** artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT) for *P. falciparum*.  
- **P. vivax:** chloroquine (where sensitive).  
- **Relapse prevention:** primaquine for *P. vivax* and *P. ovale*.  
- Treatment choice depends on parasite type, drug resistance, patient age/weight, pregnancy status.  
- Severe cases may require injectable medicines in hospital.  

## Notes
- Malaria remains a leading cause of death in Africa, especially among children.  
- Vaccines, vector control, chemoprophylaxis, and prompt treatment are key to reducing burden.
