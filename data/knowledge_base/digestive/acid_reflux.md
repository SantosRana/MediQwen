# Acid Reflux (GORD)

## Overview
- Acid reflux occurs when stomach acid travels up towards the throat.
- Heartburn is the main symptom — a burning feeling in the chest.
- Frequent reflux is called gastro‑oesophageal reflux disease (GORD).

## Symptoms
- Heartburn (burning sensation in chest)
- Sour taste in mouth
- Cough or hiccups
- Hoarse voice
- Bad breath
- Bloating, nausea
- Symptoms worse after eating, lying down, or bending over

## Causes
- Certain foods/drinks (coffee, tomatoes, alcohol, chocolate, fatty/spicy foods)
- Overweight/obesity
- Smoking
- Pregnancy
- Stress/anxiety
- Hormonal changes (progesterone, oestrogen)
- Medicines (NSAIDs like ibuprofen)
- Hiatus hernia
- Stomach ulcer
- Bacterial infection

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Eat smaller, frequent meals
- Lose weight if overweight
- Find ways to relax
- Raise head of bed 10–20 cm

**Don’t:**
- Avoid trigger foods/drinks
- Do not eat within 3–4 hours before bed
- Avoid tight clothing around waist
- Do not smoke
- Limit alcohol
- Do not stop prescribed medicines without GP advice

## Pharmacist Help
- Antacids or alginates (short‑term relief, best taken with food)
- Advice for safe medicines during pregnancy

## GP Care
See a GP if:
- Lifestyle changes and pharmacy medicines do not help
- Heartburn most days
- Other symptoms (food stuck in throat, frequent vomiting, unexplained weight loss)

**Treatment:**
- Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs): omeprazole, lansoprazole (4–8 weeks course)
- H2 receptor antagonists (e.g., famotidine) if PPIs ineffective

## Severe Cases
- Referral for specialist tests (gastroscopy, bacterial tests)
- Surgery on stomach or oesophagus if medicines fail

## Notes
- Acid reflux is common and usually manageable.
- Persistent symptoms require medical evaluation.
- Lifestyle changes + medicines are effective for most people.
