# Gastritis

## Overview
- Gastritis is inflammation of the stomach lining.
- Causes pain, indigestion, nausea, and bloating.
- Treatments include antacids, alginates, proton pump inhibitors, and antibiotics (if infection present).

## Symptoms
- Tummy pain
- Indigestion
- Feeling full/bloated
- Nausea or vomiting
- Loss of appetite
- Burping and flatulence

**Emergency (999 / A&E):**
- Vomiting blood (bright red or coffee-ground appearance)
- Black, sticky, foul-smelling stools
- Sudden severe tummy or chest pain

**Urgent GP/111:**
- Loss of appetite
- Feeling full after small meals
- Unexplained weight loss
- Lump in tummy
- Painful/difficult swallowing
- Persistent vomiting

**GP:**
- Tummy pain or indigestion >1 week
- Worsening or recurring pain

## Causes
- Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) infection
- NSAIDs (ibuprofen, aspirin)
- Excess alcohol
- Severe stress/illness (e.g., post-surgery)
- Autoimmune reaction against stomach lining

## Diagnosis
- Breath test for H. pylori
- Stool test
- Blood test
- Gastroscopy (camera test inside stomach)

## Treatments
- Antibiotics (for H. pylori)
- Acid control medicines:
  - Antacids
  - Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)
  - Alginates
- Stop NSAIDs/aspirin if possible
- Avoid alcohol if cause-related
- Referral to gastroenterologist if severe or persistent

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Reduce caffeine (tea, coffee, cola, energy drinks)
- Elevate head/shoulders in bed (extra pillow)
- Lose weight if overweight
- Discuss NSAID/aspirin use with doctor

**Don’t:**
- Avoid eating 3–4 hours before bed
- Avoid acidic, fizzy, spicy, or fatty foods/drinks
- Do not drink alcohol
- Do not smoke

## Pharmacist Help
- Antacids and alginates (short-term relief)
- PPIs (reduce acid production)
- Advice on safe medicines during pregnancy

## Complications
- Untreated gastritis may lead to stomach ulcers.
- Severe cases require specialist referral and gastroscopy.

## Notes
- Gastritis is common and often manageable.
- Identifying and treating the cause is key.
- Lifestyle changes + medicines usually control symptoms.
