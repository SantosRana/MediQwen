# Gallstones

## Overview
- Gallstones are small stones that form in the gallbladder.
- Often symptomless, but can cause pain if they block bile ducts.
- Main treatment is gallbladder removal surgery (cholecystectomy).

## Symptoms
- Usually no symptoms.
- If bile duct blocked (biliary colic):
  - Severe, constant tummy pain (lasting >30 minutes, up to hours)
  - Pain under ribs on right side or middle of tummy
  - Nausea or vomiting

**Urgent GP/111:** tummy pain lasting >30 minutes.  
**999 / A&E:** sudden severe pain, pain spreading to back with vomiting, high temperature, shivering, jaundice (yellow skin/eyes).

## Diagnosis
- Often found incidentally on scans/X-rays.
- If symptomatic: ultrasound scan, blood tests.

## Treatments
- No treatment if symptomless; monitoring may be needed.
- **If symptomatic/complications:**
  - Surgery (cholecystectomy) to remove gallbladder
  - ERCP (endoscopic retrograde cholangio-pancreatography) to clear bile duct stones

**While waiting for surgery:**
- Painkillers (paracetamol, ibuprofen; stronger if needed)
- Avoid foods that trigger symptoms
- Low-fat diet

## Complications
Rare but serious:
- Cholecystitis (gallbladder inflammation)
- Pancreatitis (inflammation of pancreas)
- Jaundice (yellow skin/eyes, itchy skin, dark urine, pale stools)

Treatment usually involves pain relief and gallbladder removal.

## Risk Factors
More likely if:
- Age >40
- Female
- Obesity
- High-fat, low-fibre diet
- Rapid weight loss (e.g., after surgery)
- Conditions such as diabetes or Crohn’s disease

## Notes
- Gallstones are common and often harmless.
- Symptomatic gallstones require medical evaluation and possible surgery.
- Lifestyle changes (healthy diet, weight management) may reduce risk.
