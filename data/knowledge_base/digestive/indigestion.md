# Indigestion (Dyspepsia)

## Overview
- Indigestion is discomfort or burning in the chest or upper abdomen after eating or drinking.
- Common and usually not serious.
- Often linked to acid reflux, diet, or lifestyle factors.

## Symptoms
- Heartburn (burning chest pain after eating)
- Feeling full and bloated
- Nausea
- Burping and flatulence
- Regurgitation (bringing up food or bitter fluids)

**Not indigestion:** stomach ache or back pain (may indicate constipation or other issues).

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Cut down on tea, coffee, cola, alcohol
- Raise head/shoulders in bed (reduce acid reflux at night)
- Lose weight if overweight

**Don’t:**
- Avoid eating 3–4 hours before bed
- Avoid rich, spicy, fatty foods
- Do not take ibuprofen/aspirin unless prescribed
- Do not smoke

## Pharmacist Help
- Medicines to ease burning/pain:
  - Antacids
  - Alginates
  - Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)
- Some medicines work best after eating; check leaflet instructions.

**Pregnancy:** indigestion common after 27 weeks due to hormones and baby pressing on stomach. Pharmacist can advise safe medicines.

## When to See a GP
- Persistent indigestion
- Severe pain
- Unexplained weight loss
- Difficulty swallowing (dysphagia)
- Persistent vomiting
- Iron deficiency anaemia
- Lump in stomach
- Bloody vomit or stool

## Causes
- Stomach acid irritating stomach lining or throat (acid reflux)
- Trigger foods/drinks (coffee, tomatoes, alcohol, chocolate, fatty/spicy foods)
- Overweight
- Smoking
- Pregnancy
- Stress/anxiety
- Medicines (NSAIDs like ibuprofen)
- Hiatus hernia
- Stomach ulcer
- Rarely: stomach cancer

## Notes
- Indigestion is usually mild and manageable.
- Persistent or severe symptoms require medical evaluation.
- Lifestyle changes and medicines are effective for most people.
