# Constipation

## Overview
- Constipation is difficulty or infrequency in passing stools.
- Common across all ages; usually manageable with diet and lifestyle changes.
- Can occasionally signal underlying medical conditions.

## Symptoms
- Fewer than 3 bowel movements per week
- Stools unusually large/small, dry, hard, or lumpy
- Straining or pain when passing stool
- Feeling of incomplete emptying
- Stomach ache, bloating, nausea
- In older adults or those with dementia/learning disabilities: may present as confusion or behavior changes

## Causes
- Low fibre diet
- Insufficient fluid intake
- Sedentary lifestyle
- Ignoring urge to defecate
- Changes in diet or routine
- Side effects of medicines
- Stress, anxiety, depression
- Pregnancy or postpartum
- Age >65
- Rarely: diverticular disease, bowel cancer

## Self Care
**Diet:**
- Eat fibre-rich foods (fruits, vegetables, cereals)
- Include sorbitol-rich fruits (apples, apricots, grapes, raspberries, strawberries)
- Drink plenty of fluids, avoid alcohol
- Add wheat bran, oats, linseed

**Routine:**
- Keep regular toilet schedule
- Do not delay urge to poo
- Use low stool to raise knees above hips when on toilet

**Activity:**
- Increase physical activity (daily walk/run)

## Pharmacist Help
- Laxatives if lifestyle changes insufficient
- Work within 3 days; short-term use only

## When to See a GP
- Constipation not improving
- Regular constipation or bloating
- Blood in stool
- Unexplained weight loss
- Persistent tiredness
- Constipation linked to medicines (e.g., opioids)
- Sudden changes in bowel habits
- Abdominal pain

## Complications
- Long-term constipation may cause faecal impaction:
  - Diarrhoea leaking around blockage
  - Treatment: stronger laxatives, suppositories, mini enemas, or manual removal by healthcare professional

## Notes
- Constipation is usually benign and treatable.
- Persistent or severe cases require medical evaluation.
- Lifestyle changes are first-line management; medicines are secondary.
