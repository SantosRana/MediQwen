# Ulcerative Colitis

## Overview
- Ulcerative colitis is a long-term inflammatory bowel disease (IBD).
- Affects the colon (large intestine) and rectum.
- Causes ulcers on the colon lining that may bleed and produce pus.
- Lifelong condition with alternating flare-ups and remission.

## Symptoms
- Recurring diarrhoea (may contain blood, mucus, pus)
- Tummy pain
- Frequent need to poo
- Fatigue
- Loss of appetite
- Weight loss

**Flare-ups:**  
- Symptoms worsen for days/weeks, then improve.  
- Extra-intestinal symptoms: arthritis, mouth ulcers, erythema nodosum (skin bumps), red/irritated eyes, osteoporosis.

## When to Seek Help
- See GP if symptoms suggest ulcerative colitis.
- Urgent GP/111 if severe flare-up suspected.
- Hospital admission may be needed for immediate care.

## Causes
- Autoimmune condition: immune system attacks colon lining.
- Likely combination of genetic and environmental factors.
- More common in white European descent, Ashkenazi Jewish communities, and black people.
- Rarer in Asian backgrounds.

## Who’s Affected
- ~1 in 227 people in UK (~296,000 cases).
- Most often diagnosed between ages 15–25.
- Affects men and women equally.

## Diagnosis
- GP assessment: symptoms, health history, physical exam.
- Tests: stool sample (infection), blood tests (anaemia, inflammation).
- Hospital tests: X-ray, CT scan, sigmoidoscopy, colonoscopy, biopsy.

## Treatments
- Aim: induce remission and maintain remission.
- **Medicines:**
  - Aminosalicylates (ASAs)
  - Corticosteroids
  - Immunosuppressants
- **Surgery:**
  - Removal of part/all colon (colectomy)
  - Ileostomy (stoma creation, sometimes temporary)
  - Ileoanal pouch (internal pouch connected to anus)
- Care team: gastroenterologists, surgeons, GPs, specialist nurses.

## Complications
- Increased risk of bowel cancer
- Poor growth in children/young people
- Osteoporosis (from steroid use)

## Notes
- Ulcerative colitis is part of IBD (with Crohn’s disease).
- Different from IBS (irritable bowel syndrome).
- Requires lifelong management and monitoring.
- Flare-ups vary in severity; treatment tailored individually.
