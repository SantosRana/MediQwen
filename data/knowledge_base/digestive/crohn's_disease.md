# Crohn’s Disease

## Overview
- Crohn’s disease is a long-term inflammatory condition of the gut.
- Can affect any part of the digestive tract (mouth, stomach, bowel, anus).
- No cure, but treatments can manage symptoms and maintain remission.
- Symptoms usually start in teens or young adults, but can occur at any age.

## Symptoms
- Diarrhoea
- Blood or mucus in stool
- Stomach pain
- Constipation
- Anal pain
- Fatigue, general malaise
- Loss of appetite, weight loss
- In children: poor growth, delayed puberty
- Extra-intestinal symptoms: joint pain, sore eyes, skin rashes
- Symptoms fluctuate: flare-ups (days to months) and remissions

## When to Seek Help
- See GP if you suspect Crohn’s disease or symptoms worsen despite treatment.
- Urgent GP/111 if:
  - Black/dark red stool
  - Bloody diarrhoea

## Diagnosis
- GP may arrange blood and stool tests.
- Referral to hospital for:
  - Endoscopy, colonoscopy, gastroscopy
  - Biopsy
  - Imaging (MRI, CT, ultrasound)
- Diagnosis often takes time due to symptom overlap with IBS, ulcerative colitis, diverticular disease, coeliac disease.

## Treatments
- **Medicines:**
  - Anti-inflammatory (steroids, mesalazine, sulfasalazine)
  - Immunosuppressants (azathioprine, mercaptopurine)
  - Biological therapies (adalimumab, infliximab)
  - Symptom relief (painkillers, laxatives, anti-diarrhoeals)
  - Enteral nutrition (liquid diet, especially for children or steroid alternatives)
- **Surgery:**
  - Bowel resection (remove damaged gut, reconnect healthy sections)
  - Ileostomy/colostomy (stoma creation)
  - Surgery for complications (strictures, fistulas, perforations)
- Surgery reduces symptoms but disease may recur elsewhere.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Eat a healthy, balanced diet (dietary advice tailored individually)
- Keep a symptom diary (food, stress, medicines)
- Stay up-to-date with vaccinations (flu, pneumococcal; avoid live vaccines if immunosuppressed)
- Carry a RADAR key for accessible toilets

**Don’t:**
- Smoke (increases flare-ups)
- Make major diet changes without medical advice
- Take NSAIDs (ibuprofen) unless prescribed

## Complications
- Fistulas (abnormal connections between bowel and other organs)
- Anal fissures
- Strictures (narrowing of bowel)
- Perforated bowel
- Increased risk of bowel cancer (may require regular colonoscopies)
- Vitamin deficiencies
- Problems with joints, bones, skin, eyes, kidneys, liver

## Life Impact
- Lifelong condition with variable severity.
- Some have mild symptoms; others experience severe complications.
- Symptoms come and go, affecting daily life differently.
- Pregnancy usually possible, but requires specialist advice (some medicines unsuitable).

## Causes
- Immune system attacks gut tissue.
- Genetic predisposition (family history).
- Environmental triggers: smoking, recent stomach bug, appendix removal.

## Notes
- Crohn’s disease requires long-term management and monitoring.
- Early diagnosis and treatment reduce complications.
- Support available from Crohn’s & Colitis UK and other patient groups.
