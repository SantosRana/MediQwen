# Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)

## Overview
- IBS is a common long-term condition affecting the digestive system.
- Causes stomach cramps, bloating, diarrhoea, and constipation.
- Symptoms come and go, lasting days to months (flare-ups and remissions).
- Lifelong condition; can significantly impact daily life.
- Diet changes and medicines often help manage symptoms.
- Exact cause unknown; linked to gut motility, gastroenteritis, oversensitivity, stress, and family history.

## Main Symptoms
- Stomach pain/cramps (worse after eating, better after pooing)
- Bloating
- Diarrhoea (watery stools, sudden urges)
- Constipation (straining, incomplete emptying)

## Triggers
- Alcohol
- Caffeine
- Spicy or fatty foods
- Stress and anxiety
- Regular antibiotic use

## Other Symptoms
- Flatulence
- Mucus in stool
- Fatigue, low energy
- Nausea
- Backache
- Urinary problems (frequency, urgency, incomplete emptying)
- Bowel incontinence
- Pelvic pain

## Diagnosis
- GP assessment: symptom history, triggers, duration.
- Tests to rule out other conditions:
  - Blood tests (e.g., coeliac disease)
  - Stool tests (infection, inflammatory bowel disease)
- Hospital tests rarely needed unless diagnosis unclear.

## Treatments
- No single cure; management tailored individually.
- **Diet & Lifestyle:**
  - Healthy balanced diet
  - Symptom diary (food, stress, medicines)
  - 8–10 drinks/day (water, non-caffeinated)
  - Relaxation techniques
  - Regular exercise
  - Probiotics trial (1 month)
- **Avoid:**
  - Skipping/delaying meals
  - Eating too quickly
  - Fatty, spicy, processed foods
  - >3 portions fresh fruit/day
  - >3 caffeinated drinks/day
  - Excess alcohol or fizzy drinks
- **Medicines:**
  - Antispasmodics (e.g., Buscopan) for cramps
  - Laxatives for constipation
  - Anti-diarrhoeals for diarrhoea
  - Low-dose antidepressants for pain/sensitivity

## Self Care for Symptoms
- Reduce bloating/cramps/flatulence:
  - Eat oats regularly
  - 1 tbsp linseeds daily
  - Avoid hard-to-digest foods (cabbage, broccoli, beans, onions)
  - Avoid sorbitol sweetener

## Notes
- IBS is not life-threatening but can greatly affect quality of life.
- Management focuses on symptom control and lifestyle adaptation.
- Support available through NHS and patient groups.
