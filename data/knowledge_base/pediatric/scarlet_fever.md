# Scarlet Fever

## Overview
- Scarlet fever is a contagious bacterial infection, mostly affecting young children.
- Easily treated with antibiotics.
- Less common in adults, but symptoms are similar.

## Symptoms
- **Early (flu-like):**
  - High temperature
  - Sore throat
  - Swollen neck glands (lump on side of neck)
- **Rash (12–48 hours later):**
  - Small, raised bumps, starting on chest/tummy then spreading
  - Skin feels rough, like sandpaper
  - Rash harder to see on brown/black skin, but bumps can be felt
- **Other signs:**
  - White coating on tongue → peels → “strawberry tongue” (red, swollen, bumpy)
  - Red cheeks (harder to see on darker skin)
  - Skin peeling as rash fades

## When to Seek Help
- **GP:** suspected scarlet fever, not better after 1 week, scarlet fever with chickenpox, relapse after recovery, contact with infected person.
- **Urgent GP/111:** worsening symptoms, complications suspected.
- **999 / A&E:** severe illness or complications (rare).

## Diagnosis
- GP can often diagnose by examining tongue and rash.
- Tests may include throat swab or blood test.

## Treatments
- **Antibiotics:** prescribed by GP.
  - Shorten illness
  - Prevent serious complications (e.g., pneumonia)
  - Reduce transmission
- **Self care:**
  - Cool fluids
  - Soft foods for sore throat
  - Paracetamol for fever/pain (no aspirin <16 years)
  - Calamine lotion or antihistamines for itching

## Duration
- Lasts ~1 week.
- Infectious until 24 hours after first antibiotic dose.
- Without antibiotics, contagious for 2–3 weeks after symptoms start.
- Stay away from school/nursery/work until 24 hours after starting antibiotics.

## Complications (rare)
- Ear infection
- Throat abscess
- Sinusitis
- Pneumonia
- Meningitis
- Rheumatic fever

## Pregnancy
- No evidence scarlet fever harms baby.
- Can make mother unwell; avoid close contact with infected people.
- Many antibiotics safe during pregnancy.

## Prevention
**Do:**
- Stay home until 24 hours after antibiotics start
- Wash hands often
- Use tissues for coughs/sneezes
- Dispose of tissues quickly

**Don’t:**
- Do not share cutlery, cups, towels, clothes, bedding, or baths

## Notes
- Scarlet fever cases have increased in recent years.
- Early antibiotic treatment prevents complications and reduces spread.
