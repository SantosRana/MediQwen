# Polio (Poliomyelitis)

## Key Facts
- Mainly affects children under 5 years.  
- 1 in 200 infections → irreversible paralysis.  
- Among paralysed, 5–10% die when breathing muscles are immobilized.  
- Wild poliovirus cases decreased by >99% since 1988 (350,000 cases → two endemic countries).  
- As long as one child remains infected, all children are at risk.  
- Failure to eradicate polio could cause global resurgence.  
- Eradication efforts strengthened surveillance and immunization systems worldwide.  

## Overview
- Highly infectious viral disease invading the nervous system.  
- Can cause total paralysis within hours.  
- Transmission: person-to-person via faecal-oral route, less often contaminated water/food.  
- Symptoms: fever, fatigue, headache, vomiting, neck stiffness, limb pain.  
- No cure; prevention through vaccination.  
- Two vaccines: oral polio vaccine (OPV) and inactivated polio vaccine (IPV).  
- Both safe and effective; used in different combinations globally.  

## Symptoms & Risk
- Polio mainly affects children <5 years, but unvaccinated individuals of any age are at risk.  
- 1 in 200 infections → irreversible paralysis (usually legs).  
- 5–10% of paralysed patients die due to respiratory muscle paralysis.  

## Eradication
- Global Polio Eradication Initiative (GPEI) launched in 1988.  
- Incidence reduced by 99%; world close to eradicating polio (second human disease after smallpox).  
- Endemic transmission persists in Afghanistan and Pakistan.  
- Failure to stop transmission in these areas risks global resurgence.  
- Eradication would save US$ 40–50 billion, mostly in low-income countries.  
- Success means no child will suffer lifelong paralysis again.  

## Notes
- Polio eradication is a major global public good.  
- Sustained implementation and resourcing of eradication strategies are critical.  
- Post-certification efforts ensure infrastructure benefits broader public health.
