# Bronchiolitis

## Overview
- Bronchiolitis is a common chest infection affecting babies and children under 2.
- Usually mild and manageable at home, but can be serious in some cases.
- Caused by viral infection, most often **respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)**.
- Different from bronchitis (which affects all ages and produces mucus cough).

## Symptoms
**Early (like a cold):**
- Sneezing
- Runny or blocked nose
- Slightly high temperature

**Progression:**
- Cough
- Rapid breathing
- Feeding difficulties
- Noisy breathing (wheezing/crackling)
- Irritability

- Symptoms peak between days 3–5, cough improves within ~3 weeks.

## When to Seek Help
- **999 / A&E:**  
  - Severe breathing difficulty (grunting, tummy sucking under ribs, rapid breathing)  
  - Breathing pauses  
  - Blue/grey skin, lips, tongue (check palms, soles, gums, eyelids in darker skin)  
  - Baby floppy/unresponsive  

- **Urgent GP/111:**  
  - Cold worsening  
  - Feeding/eating much less  
  - Dry nappy >12 hours (dehydration)  
  - High temperature (≥38°C under 3 months, ≥39°C over 3 months)  
  - Very tired, irritable, not themselves  
  - Concerned about child’s condition  

## Treatments
- No specific cure; usually resolves on its own.
- **Home care:**
  - Paracetamol (>2 months) or ibuprofen (>3 months) if distressed (not together).
  - Keep child upright when awake.
  - Monitor regularly, including at night.
  - Encourage fluids (small frequent feeds for babies, extra water/juice for older children).

**Don’t:**
- Do not smoke around child.
- Do not sponge with cool water or undress to lower temperature.
- Do not give aspirin (<16 years).

**Hospital care:**  
- Needed if child is very unwell, has underlying conditions, or not improving.  
- May include fluids, oxygen, antibiotics if secondary infection suspected.

## Prevention
- Wash hands (parent + child) often.
- Clean toys/surfaces regularly.
- Avoid sharing cups/cutlery.
- Use disposable tissues.
- Keep newborns away from people with colds/flu (especially premature or medically vulnerable babies).
- Do not smoke around children (increases severity risk).

## RSV Vaccination
- Available from 28 weeks of pregnancy (protects baby for first 6 months).
- Offered to adults aged 75–79.
- High-risk children (premature, heart/lung conditions, weakened immunity) may receive antibody injections each winter.

## Causes
- Viral infection, usually RSV.
- RSV spreads easily via coughs/sneezes.
- Almost all children get RSV before age 2.
- Higher risk of severe illness in:
  - Babies <6 months
  - Premature babies
  - Children with weakened immunity or chronic heart/lung conditions

## Notes
- Bronchiolitis is common but can be serious in vulnerable infants.
- Supportive care and monitoring are key.
- Vaccination and hygiene reduce risk.
