# Chickenpox (Varicella)

## Overview
- Chickenpox is a common, highly contagious viral infection.
- Mostly affects children, usually mild and self-limiting (1–2 weeks).
- Can be serious in newborns, pregnant women, or immunocompromised people.

## Symptoms
- **Main:** itchy, spotty rash (anywhere on body).
- **Other:** fever, aches, malaise, loss of appetite.
- Rash progresses in 3 stages:
  1. **Spots:** red/pink/darker, may appear inside mouth/genitals, harder to see on darker skin.
  2. **Blisters:** fluid-filled, itchy, may burst.
  3. **Scabs:** crusting, flaky or leaking fluid.
- New spots can appear while others are blistering/scabbing.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Fluids (ice lollies for children).
- Paracetamol for pain/fever.
- Cut nails, socks on hands at night to prevent scratching.
- Cooling creams/gels, antihistamines for itching.
- Cool baths, pat dry.
- Loose clothing.

**Don’t:**
- Avoid ibuprofen (risk of severe skin infection).
- No aspirin (<16 years).
- Do not scratch (risk of scarring).

**Important:**  
Avoid contact with newborns, pregnant women, and immunocompromised individuals.

## When to Seek Help
- **GP:** if unsure it’s chickenpox, or if breastfeeding with symptoms.
- **NHS 111:**  
  - Worsening symptoms (painful/red skin around blisters).  
  - Child shows dehydration (fewer wet nappies).  
  - Exposure with pregnancy or weakened immunity.  
  - Newborn exposed or symptomatic.  
  - Shortness of breath.  
  - Any other concerning symptoms.  
- **Urgent:** Some may need antiviral medicine within 24 hours of rash onset.

## Transmission
- Infectious from 2 days before rash until all spots scab (~5 days).
- Incubation: 1–3 weeks after exposure.
- Spread by:
  - Close contact (same room).
  - Contact with blister fluid.
  - From shingles (if never had chickenpox).

## Vaccination
- **MMRV vaccine** (measles, mumps, rubella, varicella):
  - 2 doses: at 12 months and 18 months.
  - Older children (born ≥1 Jan 2020) offered 1–2 doses.
- Offered to those at risk of spreading to immunocompromised individuals (e.g., household contacts of chemotherapy patients).

## Notes
- Chickenpox is usually mild but can cause complications in vulnerable groups.
- Vaccination reduces risk and severity.
- Supportive care is the mainstay of treatment.
