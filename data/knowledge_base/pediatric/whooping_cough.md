# Whooping Cough (Pertussis)

## Overview
- Whooping cough is a highly contagious bacterial infection of the lungs and breathing tubes.
- Can be serious, especially for babies under 12 months.
- Vaccination is essential for babies, children, and pregnant women.

## Symptoms
**Early (cold-like):**
- Runny nose
- Sore throat
- Mild cough (fever uncommon)

**After ~1 week:**
- Severe coughing bouts (worse at night, lasting minutes)
- “Whoop” sound (gasp for breath between coughs) — may be absent in infants/adults
- Breathing difficulty, turning blue/grey (infants)
- Thick mucus, vomiting
- Red face (more common in adults)
- Cough may persist for weeks or months

## When to Seek Help
- **Urgent GP/111:**  
  - Baby <6 months with symptoms  
  - Severe/worsening cough  
  - Pregnant and exposed to whooping cough  

- **999 / A&E:**  
  - Lips/skin suddenly blue/grey (check palms/soles in darker skin)  
  - Breathing difficulty (shallow breathing)  
  - Chest pain (possible pneumonia)  
  - Seizures (fits)  

## Complications
- **Babies <12 months:** dehydration, breathing problems, pneumonia, seizures.  
- **Older children/adults:** sore ribs, hernia, ear infections, urinary incontinence.

## Treatment
- **Antibiotics:** effective if started within 3 weeks of cough onset.  
  - Reduce spread, may not ease symptoms.  
  - Complete full course to prevent recurrence.  
- **Hospital care:** needed for severe cases or infants <6 months.  

**Self care:**
- Rest
- Fluids
- Paracetamol/ibuprofen for discomfort (check suitability)
- No aspirin (<16 years)

## Contagious Period
- Up to 3 weeks after cough starts.
- Antibiotics within 2 weeks reduce contagious period.
- Stay off school/work/nursery until:
  - 48 hours after starting antibiotics, OR
  - 3 weeks after cough onset (if no antibiotics).

## Vaccination
- **Routine NHS schedule:**
  - **6-in-1 vaccine:** at 8, 12, 16 weeks, and 18 months.
  - **4-in-1 booster:** at 3 years 4 months.
- **Pregnancy:** vaccine recommended around 20 weeks to protect newborn in first weeks of life.

## Notes
- Whooping cough remains a serious infection despite vaccination.
- Early diagnosis and treatment reduce complications and spread.
- Vaccination is the most effective prevention strategy.
