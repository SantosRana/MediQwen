# Croup

## Overview
- Croup is a common condition affecting babies’ and young children’s airways.
- Usually mild, but can sometimes require medical treatment.
- Symptoms often worse at night and follow cold-like illness.

## Symptoms
- Barking cough (seal-like sound)
- Hoarse voice
- Difficulty breathing
- High-pitched rasping sound when breathing in (stridor)
- Cold-like symptoms: fever, runny nose, cough
- Symptoms develop after a few days, peak at night.

## When to Seek Help
- **Urgent GP/111:**  
  - Suspected croup  
  - Symptoms worsening or not improving after GP visit  
  - Children <3 months with croup symptoms  

- **999 / A&E:**  
  - Struggling to breathe (grunting, tummy sucking under ribs, noisy breathing)  
  - Blue/grey/pale/blotchy skin, lips, tongue (check palms/soles in darker skin)  
  - Sleepier than normal, difficult to wake  
  - Unusually quiet, still, limp, floppy, unresponsive  
  - Very upset/restless, cannot be calmed, difficulty breathing  
  - Drooling more than usual, difficulty swallowing, prefers sitting upright  

## Home Care (Mild Croup)
**Do:**
- Sit child upright, avoid lying down
- Comfort and keep calm (crying worsens symptoms)
- Give plenty of fluids
- Monitor regularly, including at night
- Paracetamol or ibuprofen for fever/discomfort

**Don’t:**
- Do not use steam or steamy rooms

## Causes
- Viral infection causing swelling of the voice box (larynx) and windpipe (trachea).
- Narrowed airways lead to barking cough and stridor.

## Outlook
- Usually resolves within 48 hours.
- Most cases mild, but severe cases can be life-threatening if untreated.
- Early medical advice ensures safe management.

## Notes
- Parents should trust their instincts if child seems seriously unwell.
- Hospital referral may be needed for severe cases.
