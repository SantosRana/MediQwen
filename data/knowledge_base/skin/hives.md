# Hives (Urticaria)

## Overview
- Hives is a skin reaction causing itchy raised rashes.
- Usually clears within a few days.
- Often treatable at home with antihistamines.
- Can be triggered by allergens, irritants, or other factors.

## Symptoms
- Itchy rash with raised bumps or patches (varied shapes/sizes).
- Can appear anywhere on body, localized or widespread.
- Rash may sting or burn.
- **On white skin:** pink/red.  
- **On brown/black skin:** colour change less visible, but raised rash can be felt/seen.

## When to Seek Help
- **Pharmacist:** advice on antihistamines (not always suitable for young children or those with long-term conditions).  
- **Urgent GP/111:**  
  - Rash not improving after 2 days.  
  - Rash spreading.  
  - Recurrent hives (possible allergy).  
  - Associated fever/unwellness.  
  - Swelling under skin (angioedema).  
- **999 / A&E:**  
  - Sudden swelling of lips, mouth, throat, tongue.  
  - Breathing difficulty, choking, wheezing.  
  - Tight throat, difficulty swallowing.  
  - Skin/lips/tongue turning blue/grey/pale (check palms/soles in darker skin).  
  - Confusion, drowsiness, dizziness, fainting, unresponsiveness.  
  - Child limp/floppy, not responding normally.  
- These may indicate a **serious allergic reaction (anaphylaxis)**.

## Treatment
- **GP:** may prescribe menthol cream, antihistamines, or steroid tablets.  
- Referral to dermatologist if persistent.  
- Most cases improve with antihistamines and self-care.

## Causes
- Triggered by release of histamine and other chemicals in skin.  
- **Common triggers:**  
  - Certain foods.  
  - Plants, animals, chemicals, latex.  
  - Cold (water, wind).  
  - Heat, sweat, exercise, stress, spicy food.  
  - Medicines, insect bites/stings.  
  - Scratching/pressure (tight clothing).  
  - Infections.  
  - Immune system problems.  
  - Rarely: water or sunlight.  

## Prevention
- Identify and avoid triggers where possible.  
- Keep track of episodes to spot patterns.  
- Use antihistamines when appropriate.

## Notes
- Hives is usually mild and self-limiting.  
- Serious allergic reactions require immediate emergency care.
