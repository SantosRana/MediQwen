# Psoriasis

## Overview
- Psoriasis is a common, chronic skin condition causing itchy, sore, flaky patches.
- Cannot be cured, but treatments help manage symptoms.
- Symptoms often start between ages 15–25 or 50–60.
- Flare-ups alternate with periods of improvement.

## Symptoms
- Dry, itchy, sore, flaky patches with silvery-white scales.
- Patches: 1–2 cm, oval/irregular.
- **On white skin:** pink/red → silvery-white, sometimes yellow/orange-brown.  
- **On brown/black skin:** grey, leaving dark marks after healing.
- Common sites: scalp, behind ears, chest, tummy, back, bottom, elbows, knees, groin, genitals, under breasts, fingernails (pitting).
- May cause small brown/yellow spots on palms/soles.
- Can lead to **psoriatic arthritis** (joint pain, stiffness, swelling).

## When to Seek Help
- **GP:** suspected psoriasis, ineffective treatment, side effects, joint pain/swelling.
- **999 / A&E:** sudden widespread redness, pus-filled bumps, yellow-brown pus patches on feet, red scaly pus bumps on fingers/toes, plus systemic symptoms (fast heartbeat, fever, chills).

## Causes
- Overproduction of skin cells → scaly patches.
- Linked to immune system dysfunction.
- Family history increases risk.
- **Triggers:** stress, alcohol, smoking, obesity, pregnancy, certain medicines, infections (strep A, HIV), skin injury, sunlight.

## Treatments
- **Topical:** vitamin D creams, emollients, steroid creams, coal tar.
- **Severe cases:** light therapy, systemic medicines.
- Combination therapy often needed.
- GP or dermatologist may prescribe.
- Mental health support if condition affects wellbeing.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Avoid triggers.
- Healthy diet, regular exercise.
- Reduce stress.
- Limit alcohol.
- Stop smoking.

**Don’t:**
- Share emollients.
- Dip fingers into emollient pots (use spoon/pump).
- Smoke or go near flames with emollient (risk of fire).

## Complications
- Psoriatic arthritis.
- Metabolic syndrome (diabetes, cardiovascular disease, DVT).
- Inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn’s).

## Pregnancy
- Some treatments unsafe in pregnancy.
- Symptoms may worsen during pregnancy.
- Consult doctor/pharmacist if pregnant or planning pregnancy.

## Notes
- Psoriasis is not contagious.
- Regular monitoring helps manage complications.
- Support available from Psoriasis Association and NHS Better Health.
