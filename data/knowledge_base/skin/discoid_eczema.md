# Discoid Eczema (Nummular Dermatitis)

## Overview
- Chronic skin condition causing itchy, swollen, cracked patches in circular or oval shapes.
- Can last weeks, months, or years without treatment.
- Often recurs in the same areas.

## Symptoms
- Distinct circular/oval patches, usually not on face or scalp.
- Start as small spots/bumps → merge into larger patches (few mm to several cm).
- **On lighter skin:** pink/red.  
- **On darker skin:** dark brown or paler than surrounding skin.  
- Early patches: swollen, blistered, oozing fluid, very itchy (especially at night).  
- Later patches: dry, crusty, cracked, flaky.  
- Centre may clear, leaving ring-like appearance (can resemble ringworm).  
- Multiple patches common; skin between often dry.  
- Infection signs: excessive oozing, yellow crust, hot/swollen/painful skin, fever, chills, feeling unwell.

## When to Seek Help
- **Pharmacist/GP:** suspected discoid eczema, troubling symptoms.  
- **GP:** suspected infection (may need antibiotic cream or tablets).  
- **Dermatologist referral:** uncertain diagnosis, patch testing.

## Causes
- Exact cause unknown; linked to very dry skin reducing barrier function.
- Possible role of contact dermatitis (irritants in soaps, toiletries).
- Associated with history of atopic eczema, asthma, hay fever (though not familial like atopic eczema).
- **Triggers:** minor skin injuries (insect bites, burns), certain medicines, dry/cold environments.  
- Symptoms may improve in sunny or humid climates.

## Treatment
- **Main treatments:**
  - Emollients (moisturisers).
  - Topical corticosteroids (steroid creams/ointments).
  - Antihistamines (reduce itching).
- **Additional:** antibiotics if infected, stronger medicines for severe cases.
- Lifestyle: avoid irritating chemicals (soaps, detergents, bubble baths, shower gels).

## Notes
- Discoid eczema is long-term but manageable with treatment.
- May leave permanent skin discoloration after healing.
