# Atopic Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis)

## Overview
- Common chronic skin condition causing itchy, dry, inflamed skin.
- Affects all ages, most common in children.
- Cannot be cured, but symptoms can be managed.
- Symptoms often improve with age, though flare-ups occur.

## Symptoms
- Itchy, dry, cracked, crusty, scaly, or thickened skin.
- Red, white, purple, grey, lighter or darker than surrounding skin (varies by skin tone).
- May blister or bleed.
- Common sites: elbows, knees, hands; in babies/toddlers, face.
- Symptoms start at any age, usually in childhood.
- Flare-ups alternate with periods of improvement.

## When to Seek Help
- **GP:** suspected eczema, ineffective treatment.
- **Urgent GP/111:**  
  - Blistered, crusty, leaking fluid, pus-filled spots.  
  - Painful, swollen, warm skin.  
  - Sudden worsening or spreading.  
  - High temperature or feeling unwell.  
- These may indicate infection or **eczema herpeticum**.

## Causes
- Exact cause unknown.
- More likely if parents have eczema, or family history of asthma/hay fever.
- **Triggers:** allergens/irritants (soap, detergents, pets, fabrics, pollen, dust mites, foods), heat, temperature changes, infections (e.g., staph), stress, hormonal changes (pregnancy).
- Not contagious.

## Treatments
- **Main:**  
  - Emollients (moisturising creams, lotions, ointments, gels).  
  - Topical corticosteroids (steroid creams, lotions, gels).  
- **Other:** special dressings, medicines for severe cases.
- Pharmacist can advise for mild eczema; GP for more severe cases.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Avoid triggers (soap, detergents, fabrics, pets).
- Apply emollients frequently (≥2 times daily).
- Wash with emollients instead of soap.
- Keep cool.
- Keep nails short; use mittens for babies.

**Don’t:**
- Do not scratch (rub gently if itchy).
- Do not share emollients.
- Do not dip fingers into emollient pots (use spoon/pump).
- Do not use aqueous cream (can irritate).
- Do not change diet unless advised by doctor.
- Do not smoke or go near flames with emollient (risk of fabric fire).

## Notes
- Atopic eczema is the most common type, but other forms exist: contact dermatitis, discoid eczema, pompholyx, varicose eczema.
- Support available from Allergy UK and NHS resources.
