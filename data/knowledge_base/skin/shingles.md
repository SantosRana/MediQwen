# Shingles (Herpes Zoster)

## Overview
- Shingles is an infection caused by reactivation of the chickenpox virus.
- Produces a painful rash, usually on one side of the body.
- Not contagious as shingles, but can transmit chickenpox to those unvaccinated or never infected.

## Symptoms
- Early: tingling/painful skin, headache, feeling unwell.
- Rash appears a few days later (sometimes pain without rash).
- Rash: blotches → itchy blisters → ooze fluid → dry/scab after days.
- Usually on chest/tummy, but can appear on face, eyes, genitals.
- Rash only on one side of body (bilateral rash unlikely shingles).
- Pain may persist after rash heals.

## When to Seek Help
- **Pharmacist:** adults ≥18 with symptoms (within 3 days of rash for antiviral benefit).
- **Urgent GP/111:**  
  - Pregnant or breastfeeding (rash on breasts).  
  - Rash on eye/nose, vision changes.  
  - Severely weakened immune system (e.g., chemotherapy).  
  - Age ≤17.  
- **999 / A&E:** severe complications (e.g., breathing difficulty, neurological symptoms).

## Treatment
- Depends on severity and risk of complications.
- **Antiviral tablets:** within 3 days of rash onset, especially for severe cases or immunocompromised.
- **Pain relief:** paracetamol, steroid tablets, nerve pain medicines.
- **Self care:**  
  - Keep rash clean/dry.  
  - Wear loose clothing.  
  - Use cool compresses.  
  - Avoid adhesive dressings or rough fabrics.

## Duration
- Rash heals in ~4 weeks.
- Pain may last weeks/months (post-herpetic neuralgia).

## Contagiousness
- Cannot spread shingles itself.
- Can spread chickenpox to unvaccinated/uninfected individuals while rash is oozing.
- Avoid contact with: pregnant women (no prior chickenpox), immunocompromised people, babies <1 month.
- Stay off work/school if rash cannot be covered or is still oozing.

## Complications
- Post-herpetic neuralgia (persistent pain).
- Secondary infection of rash (may need antibiotics).
- Scarring or skin colour changes.
- Muscle weakness.
- Eye problems (possible sight loss).
- Ramsay Hunt syndrome (facial nerve involvement, hearing problems).

## Causes
- Reactivation of dormant chickenpox virus (varicella-zoster).
- Triggered by weakened immune system (stress, illness, chemotherapy).

## Vaccination
- NHS shingles vaccine available for:  
  - People turning 65 on/after 1 Sept 2023.  
  - People aged 70–79 not yet vaccinated.  
  - People ≥18 with severely weakened immune system.  
- Vaccine reduces risk and severity.
- Important even if previously had shingles.

## Notes
- Shingles can recur.
- Early treatment reduces complications.
- Vaccination is key prevention strategy.
