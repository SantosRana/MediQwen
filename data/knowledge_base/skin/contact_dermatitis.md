# Contact Dermatitis

## Overview
- Contact dermatitis is a type of eczema triggered by contact with a substance (irritant or allergen).
- Causes dry, itchy, irritated skin.
- Usually improves or clears up if the trigger is identified and avoided.
- Treatments available to ease symptoms.

## Symptoms
- Itchy, blistered, dry, cracked skin.
- **On lighter skin:** red rash.  
- **On darker skin:** dark brown, purple, or grey rash.  
- Reaction occurs within hours or days of exposure.
- Commonly affects hands and face, but can occur anywhere.

## When to Seek Help
- **Pharmacist:** troubling symptoms, advice on emollients.  
- **GP:** persistent, recurrent, or severe symptoms.  
- **Dermatologist referral:** if cause cannot be identified or symptoms resistant to treatment.

## Causes
- **Irritant dermatitis:** direct damage to skin (soaps, detergents, solvents, frequent water contact).  
- **Allergic dermatitis:** immune reaction to allergens (cosmetics, nickel, raw fruit/vegetables).  
- Allergic type less common than irritant type.

## Diagnosis
- GP assessment based on history and symptoms.
- Dermatologist may perform patch testing to identify allergens.

## Treatment
- Avoid irritants/allergens where possible.
- **Main treatments:**
  - Emollients (moisturisers).
  - Topical corticosteroids (steroid creams/ointments).
- Rarely: oral corticosteroids for severe widespread cases.

## Prevention
- Avoid known triggers.
- Rinse skin promptly with warm water + emollient after exposure.
- Use protective gloves (with cotton liners if rubber irritates).
- Switch to non-irritant products (check ingredients).
- Apply emollients frequently and generously.
- Use emollient soap substitutes instead of regular soaps.

## Notes
- Contact dermatitis is not contagious.
- Identifying and avoiding triggers is the most effective management strategy.
