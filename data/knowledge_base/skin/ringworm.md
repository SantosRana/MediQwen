# Ringworm (Tinea)

## Overview
- Ringworm is a common fungal infection, not caused by worms.
- Usually mild and treatable with antifungal medicines from a pharmacy.
- Can affect anyone, including children.

## Symptoms
- Rash: red or darker than surrounding skin (depending on skin tone).
- Rash may be scaly, dry, swollen, itchy.
- Typically ring-shaped, but may look different on face, neck, or scalp.
- Can appear anywhere: body, scalp (**tinea capitis**), groin (**jock itch**).
- Rash may spread or multiply.
- Scalp/face involvement may cause patchy hair loss.

## When to Seek Help
- **Pharmacist:** first point of contact, can recommend antifungal creams, gels, sprays, or tablets.  
- **GP:**  
  - Rash not improving after pharmacy treatment.  
  - Ringworm on scalp (needs prescription antifungal tablets + shampoo).  
  - Weakened immune system (chemotherapy, steroids, diabetes).  

## Treatment
- Antifungal medicines (daily use for up to 4 weeks).  
- Important to complete full course even if rash clears early.  
- GP may prescribe stronger antifungals for scalp or recurrent cases.

## Causes
- Caused by fungi.  
- Spread via close contact with:  
  - Infected people or animals.  
  - Contaminated objects (bedsheets, combs, towels).  
  - Soil (less common).  

## Prevention
**Do:**
- Start treatment promptly.  
- Wash towels and bedsheets regularly.  
- Keep skin clean, wash hands after touching animals/soil.  
- Check skin regularly after exposure.  
- Take pets to vet if they show fur loss (possible ringworm).  

**Don’t:**
- Do not share towels, combs, or bedding.  
- Do not scratch rash (spreads infection).  

## Notes
- Children can attend school/nursery once treatment has started.  
- Ringworm is contagious but easily managed with antifungal therapy.
