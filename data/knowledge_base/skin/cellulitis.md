# Cellulitis

## Overview
- Cellulitis is a bacterial skin infection treated with antibiotics.
- Can be serious if not treated quickly.
- Most common on lower limbs, but can affect any body part.
- Not contagious (affects deeper skin layers).

## Symptoms
- Painful, hot, swollen skin.
- Redness (less obvious on brown/black skin).
- Blisters may form.
- Flu-like symptoms: fever, swollen painful glands.
- Common sites: legs, feet (often linked to athlete’s foot), hands, around eyes (serious).
- Severe cases: purple patches, systemic illness.

## When to Seek Help
- **Urgent GP/111:** painful, hot, swollen skin.
- **999 / A&E:**  
  - Very high temperature, chills.  
  - Fast heartbeat/breathing.  
  - Purple skin patches.  
  - Dizziness, fainting, confusion.  
  - Cold, clammy, pale skin.  
  - Unresponsiveness or loss of consciousness.  

## Treatment
- **Mild cases:** oral antibiotics (usually 1 week).  
  - Symptoms may worsen first 48 hours, then improve.  
  - Complete full course.  
  - Recovery: 7–10 days.  
- **Severe cases:** hospital treatment.  
- **Recurrent cellulitis:** low-dose long-term antibiotics.

**Self care:**
- Paracetamol/ibuprofen for pain.
- Elevate affected limb to reduce swelling.
- Move nearby joints to prevent stiffness.
- Drink fluids to avoid dehydration.
- Avoid compression stockings until recovered.

## Prevention
- Keep skin clean and moisturised.
- Clean cuts/wounds, use antiseptic cream.
- Wear protective clothing/footwear outdoors.
- Wear gloves for outdoor work.

## Causes
- Bacterial infection enters deeper skin layers via breaks (cuts, bites, cracks).
- Risk factors: poor circulation (obesity), immobility, weakened immunity (chemotherapy, diabetes), lymphoedema, drug injection, surgical wounds, previous cellulitis.

## Complications
- Untreated cellulitis can spread to blood, muscles, bones.
- Life-threatening if not managed promptly.

## Notes
- Early antibiotic treatment is crucial.
- Most people recover fully with proper care.
