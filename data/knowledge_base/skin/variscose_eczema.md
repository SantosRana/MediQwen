# Varicose Eczema (Stasis/Gravitational Eczema)

## Overview
- Long-term skin condition affecting lower legs.
- Common in people with varicose veins.
- Chronic but manageable with treatment.

## Symptoms
- Itchy, swollen, dry, flaky, scaly, crusty, or blistered skin.
- **On white skin:** red or brown.  
- **On brown/black skin:** dark brown, purple, or grey (sometimes harder to see).  
- Symptoms fluctuate (better/worse periods).
- Legs may swell, especially after standing long periods.
- Visible varicose veins often present.
- Other signs: tender/tight skin (lipodermatosclerosis), small white scars (atrophie blanche), pain, eczema elsewhere.
- Untreated cases may lead to **leg ulcers**.

## When to Seek Help
- **GP:** suspected varicose eczema.  
- GP may check for venous problems (varicose veins, DVT, leg ulcers, cellulitis, past surgery/injury).  
- May perform ankle brachial pressure index (ABPI) test before prescribing compression stockings.  
- **Referral:** vascular specialist or dermatologist if needed.

## Causes
- Increased pressure in leg veins due to faulty valves → blood leaks backwards.  
- Fluid leaks into tissue → immune reaction → eczema.  
- More common with varicose veins.  
- **Risk factors:** female sex, obesity, pregnancy, family history of venous insufficiency, immobility, previous DVT, older age.

## Treatment
- **Self-help:** improve circulation (stay active, raise legs).  
- **Medical:**  
  - Emollients (moisturisers).  
  - Topical corticosteroids (creams/ointments).  
  - Compression stockings (if ABPI test safe).  
- Combination therapy often required.

## Notes
- Chronic condition but controllable with lifestyle + medical treatment.
- Preventing progression reduces risk of leg ulcers.
