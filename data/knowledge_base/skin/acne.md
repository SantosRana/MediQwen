# Acne

## Overview
- Acne is a common skin condition affecting most people at some point.
- Causes spots, oily skin, and sometimes painful or hot skin.
- Most common in teenagers and young adults, but can occur at any age.
- Usually improves in mid-20s, though some adults continue to have acne.

## Symptoms
- **Common sites:** face (almost all cases), back (>50%), chest (~15%).
- **Types of spots:**
  - Blackheads: small dark bumps (not dirt, caused by pigment in follicle lining).
  - Whiteheads: firm bumps, do not empty when squeezed.
  - Papules: small red tender bumps.
  - Pustules: papules with white pus tip.
  - Nodules: large painful lumps under skin.
  - Cysts: severe pus-filled lumps, risk of scarring.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Wash affected skin ≤2 times daily with mild cleanser and lukewarm water.
- Use water-based, non-comedogenic products.
- Remove make-up before bed.
- Use fragrance-free emollients if skin is dry.
- Exercise for mood/self-esteem, shower promptly after.
- Wash hair regularly, avoid hair across face.

**Don’t:**
- Avoid squeezing/picking spots (causes scarring).
- Avoid oil-based products.
- Do not overwash (irritates skin).

## When to Seek Help
- **Pharmacist:** mild acne, advice on creams/gels (e.g., benzoyl peroxide).
- **GP:** moderate/severe acne, nodules/cysts, acne on chest/back, ineffective OTC treatment, or if acne affects mental health.
- Treatments may take months to work but results are usually good.

## Causes
- Linked to hormonal changes (puberty, menstrual cycle, pregnancy).
- Hormones increase sebum production → changes *P. acnes* bacteria → inflammation/pus.
- Thickened follicle lining → blocked pores.
- **Other factors:** family history, hormonal fluctuations.
- Not caused by poor hygiene or sexual activity.

## Who’s Affected
- ~95% of people aged 11–30 affected.
- Most common in girls (14–17) and boys (16–19).
- Often lasts several years, improves with age.
- ~3% of adults >35 still affected.

## Notes
- Acne cannot be cured but can be controlled.
- Early treatment prevents scarring.
- Emotional impact significant; support may be needed.
