# Scabies

## Overview
- Scabies is an itchy rash caused by mites burrowing into the skin.
- Spread through close skin-to-skin contact (including sexual contact).
- Needs prompt treatment to stop spreading.
- Not transmitted from pets.

## Symptoms
- Intense itching, especially at night.
- Raised rash or spots (red on lighter skin, harder to see on darker skin but can be felt).
- Mite burrows: lines with a dot at one end.
- Common sites: between fingers, wrists, under arms, waist, groin, bottom.
- In infants/elderly: may affect head, neck, palms, soles.
- **Crusted scabies (rare, highly contagious):** crusted, flaky rash on elbows, knees, hands, feet (seen in immunocompromised).

## When to Seek Help
- **Pharmacist:** most cases, can recommend cream/lotion for whole body (excluding eyes).
- **GP:**  
  - Children <2 years old.  
  - Suspected infection or coexisting skin condition (eczema).  
  - Crusted scabies.  
  - Persistent itching 2–4 weeks after treatment.  

## Treatment
- Cream/lotion applied over entire body (including scalp/face, avoiding eyes).
- Repeat treatment after 7 days.
- Everyone in household treated simultaneously, even if asymptomatic.
- Sexual contacts in past 8 weeks should also be treated.
- Children <2 years: GP treatment required.
- Itching may persist for weeks after mites are killed.

## Prevention
**Do:**
- Wash bedding/clothing at ≥60°C and hot dry on first day of treatment.
- Seal unwashable clothing in bag for ≥3 days.

**Don’t:**
- Avoid sex or close contact until treatment completed.
- Do not share bedding, clothing, towels.

## Duration
- Adults/children ≥5 years: return to work/school after starting treatment (avoid close contact for 24 hours).
- Children <5 years: return to nursery/preschool 24 hours after first treatment.

## Notes
- Scabies is very infectious, especially in group living environments (nurseries, halls, nursing homes).
- Prompt treatment prevents spread and complications.
