# Impetigo

## Overview
- Impetigo is a highly contagious bacterial skin infection.
- More common in young children, but anyone can get it.
- Not usually serious; clears quickly with treatment.

## Symptoms
- Starts with red sores or blisters (redness less visible on brown/black skin).
- Sores/blisters burst → golden-brown crusty patches (cornflake-like).
- Patches may spread, be itchy, sometimes painful.
- Common sites: face (around nose/mouth), hands.
- **Bullous impetigo:** blisters ooze yellow liquid → scaly border around scab.

## When to Seek Help
- **Pharmacist:** children ≥12 months, mild symptoms.  
- **GP:**  
  - Child <12 months.  
  - Breastfeeding with sores on breasts.  
  - Weakened immune system (e.g., chemotherapy).  
  - Symptoms worsening or recurring.  
  - Recurrent impetigo within past year.  
- GP may suggest phone consultation due to contagiousness.

## Treatment
- **Pharmacist/GP:** check for cellulitis or other infections.  
- **Medications:**  
  - Hydrogen peroxide cream.  
  - Antibiotic cream or tablets.  
- Babies/immunocompromised: oral antibiotics to prevent complications.  
- Recurrent cases: swabs (skin/nose), antiseptic body wash, nasal ointment.

**Important:**  
- Complete full course of treatment (cream or tablets).  
- Symptoms usually improve within a few days.

## Contagious Period
- Stops being contagious:  
  - 48 hours after starting prescribed cream/antibiotics.  
  - Or when patches dry/crust over (without treatment).  

## Prevention
**Do:**
- Stay away from school/work/nursery until no longer contagious.  
- Wash affected areas with soap/water, dry thoroughly.  
- Wash hands frequently (before/after applying cream).  
- Wash towels, sheets, pillowcases at high temperature.  
- Clean toys with detergent/warm water if children affected.  

**Don’t:**
- Avoid touching/scratching sores (prevents scarring).  
- Avoid close contact with children, diabetics, or immunocompromised people.  
- Do not share towels, sheets, pillowcases.  
- Do not prepare food for others.  
- Avoid gym/contact sports until cleared.

## Causes
- Bacteria infect damaged skin (cuts, scratches, insect bites, eczema, scabies).
- Spread via skin-to-skin contact or contaminated items (towels, bedding).

## Notes
- Impetigo is very contagious but usually mild.
- Early treatment prevents spread and complications.
