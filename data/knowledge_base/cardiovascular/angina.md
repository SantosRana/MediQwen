# Angina

## Overview
- Angina is chest pain or tightness due to reduced blood flow to the heart.
- Often triggered by exercise, stress, emotion, or cold temperatures.
- Indicates underlying heart disease and increases risk of heart attack or stroke.

## Symptoms
- Sudden pain in chest, neck, shoulders, jaw, or arms
- Pain feels like tightness, squeezing, pressure, or dull ache
- May cause nausea, breathlessness, dizziness, or sweating
- Attacks usually last <10 minutes and improve with rest or medicine

## Treating an Angina Attack
- Stop activity and rest
- Use prescribed medicine (usually glyceryl trinitrate, GTN)
- Repeat dose after 5 minutes if needed
- If symptoms persist after 2 doses (10 minutes), call 999

## Emergency (999 / A&E)
Call immediately if:
- Chest pain does not stop after rest or medicine
- Sudden chest pain feels like squeezing, burning, or indigestion
- Pain spreads to arm, neck, jaw, shoulder, stomach, or back
- Chest pain with sweating, nausea, dizziness, or breathlessness  
**Possible heart attack — urgent hospital treatment required.**

## NHS 111 / GP
Seek help if:
- Chest pain comes and goes
- Angina symptoms worsen, occur more often, last longer, or happen at rest

## GP Appointment
- Referral to cardiologist if angina suspected
- Tests may include: blood tests, ECG, oxygen level check, angiogram, MRI, CT scan

## Causes
- Reduced blood supply to heart (usually coronary heart disease)
- Narrowed/blocked arteries due to fatty deposits (atherosclerosis)
- Vasospastic or microvascular angina (blood vessel tightening)

**Risk factors:**
- Age (men ≥45, women ≥55)
- Smoking
- Excess alcohol (>14 units/week)
- Obesity/overweight
- High blood pressure or cholesterol
- Previous heart problems (heart attack, arrhythmia)
- Family history of heart disease
- Other conditions (diabetes, arthritis, kidney disease)

## Treatments
- **Medicines:**
  - GTN for attacks
  - Beta blockers to prevent attacks
  - Aspirin/statins to reduce risk of stroke/heart attack
- **Procedures:**
  - Coronary angioplasty
  - Coronary artery bypass surgery

## Prevention & Lifestyle
**Do:**
- Avoid triggers (stress, cold, large meals)
- Always carry angina medicine
- Quit smoking
- Maintain healthy weight
- Eat balanced diet
- Stay active within limits
- Reduce alcohol (<14 units/week)

## Impact on Life
- Higher risk of heart attack or stroke
- With treatment, symptoms often improve or stop
- Usually does not prevent normal activities if controlled
- May affect jobs with heavy physical activity or driving buses/coaches/lorries
- Carry spare medicine and plan ahead when travelling

## Notes
- Angina is a warning sign of heart disease.
- Proper treatment and lifestyle changes reduce risks.
- Controlled angina allows most people to live active lives.
