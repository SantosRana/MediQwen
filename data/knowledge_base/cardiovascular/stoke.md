# Stroke

## Overview
- A stroke occurs when blood supply to part of the brain is cut off.
- Symptoms appear suddenly and require urgent medical attention.
- Types: ischaemic (blood clot), haemorrhagic (bleeding), transient ischaemic attack (TIA or mini-stroke).

## Symptoms
**Main FAST signs:**
- **Face:** drooping on one side, difficulty smiling
- **Arms:** weakness or numbness, unable to lift both arms
- **Speech:** slurred, confused, or unable to speak
- **Time:** call 999 immediately

**Other symptoms:**
- Weakness/numbness on one side
- Blurred or lost vision
- Difficulty speaking or thinking
- Confusion, memory loss
- Dizziness, falls
- Severe headache
- Nausea or vomiting

**Note:** Symptoms may stop after a short time (TIA). Still seek immediate help.

## Diagnosis
Tests may include:
- Blood tests
- CT, MRI, ultrasound scans
- ECG (to check heart rhythm)

## Treatment
Depends on stroke type:
- **First 24 hours:**
  - Thrombolysis (clot-busting medicine)
  - Thrombectomy (clot removal surgery)
  - Procedures to relieve brain pressure
- **Hospital care:** multidisciplinary team for recovery
- **Long-term medicines:**
  - Anticoagulants (prevent clots)
  - Blood pressure medication
  - Statins (lower cholesterol)

## Causes & Risk Factors
Stroke risk increases with:
- Age >50
- Black or South Asian background
- Sickle cell disease
- Migraines
- Combined contraceptive pill
- Pregnancy with pre-eclampsia or recent childbirth
- Unhealthy lifestyle

**Medical conditions:**
- High blood pressure
- Diabetes
- Atrial fibrillation
- High cholesterol
- Previous TIA

## Prevention
**Do:**
- Quit smoking
- Eat a balanced diet
- Exercise regularly
- Limit alcohol

**Don’t:**
- Skip medicines for underlying conditions (e.g., hypertension, diabetes)

## Notes
- Stroke is a medical emergency — immediate treatment saves lives.
- TIA is a warning sign and must be treated urgently.
- Lifestyle changes and medicines reduce risk of recurrence.
