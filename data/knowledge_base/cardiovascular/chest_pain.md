# Chest Pain

## Emergency Signs
Call 911 / emergency services immediately if:
- Sudden chest pain or discomfort that does not go away (squeezing, pressure, burning, indigestion-like)
- Pain spreading to arm, neck, jaw, stomach, or back
- Chest pain with sweating, nausea, lightheadedness, or shortness of breath

These may indicate a **heart attack** requiring immediate hospital treatment.

## When to See a Doctor (Non-Emergency)
See a GP if:
- Chest pain comes and goes
- Chest pain goes away quickly but you remain concerned

Medical advice is important to rule out serious causes.

## Common Causes
Chest pain often has non-cardiac causes. Examples:

| Symptom Pattern | Possible Cause |
|-----------------|----------------|
| After eating, reflux, bloating | Heartburn or indigestion |
| After chest injury/exercise, worse on breathing in, relieved by rest | Chest sprain or strain |
| Sharp, continuous pain with anxiety, fast heartbeat, sweating, dizziness | Anxiety, depression, panic attack |
| Worse on breathing in, cough with yellow/green mucus, fever | Chest infection, pneumonia, pleurisy |
| Tingling skin, rash → blisters | Shingles |

## Heart-Related Causes
- **Pericarditis**: Sudden, sharp, stabbing pain, worse on deep breathing or lying down  
- **Angina or Heart Attack**: Similar symptoms, but heart attack is life-threatening  

### Risk Factors for Coronary Heart Disease
- Smoking  
- Obesity  
- High blood pressure, diabetes, or high cholesterol  
- Family history of heart attacks/angina under age 60  

## Notes
- Most chest pain is not serious, but always rule out cardiac causes first  
- Risk increases with age and cardiovascular risk factors  
- Do not self-diagnose — seek medical advice if unsure
