# Cardiomyopathy

## Overview
- Cardiomyopathy is a disease of the heart muscle where walls of the heart chambers become stretched, thickened, or stiff.
- Affects the heart’s ability to pump blood effectively.
- Can be inherited, caused by other conditions, or sometimes unknown.
- Can affect people of all ages.

## Types

### Dilated Cardiomyopathy
- Heart muscle walls stretched and thin → weak contraction.
- Risks: heart failure, valve problems, arrhythmias, blood clots.
- Causes: genetic mutations, high blood pressure, poor diet, alcohol/drugs, viral infections, valve disease, autoimmune/vascular diseases, pregnancy (rare).
- Affects both children and adults.

### Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy
- Heart muscle cells enlarge, walls thicken → reduced chamber size, stiff walls.
- May obstruct blood flow, cause mitral valve leakage, endocarditis risk.
- Symptoms: dizziness, chest pain, breathlessness, fainting.
- Leading cause of sudden death in young athletes.
- Often inherited; affects ~1 in 500 people in UK.

### Restrictive Cardiomyopathy
- Rare, often diagnosed in children.
- Walls stiff and rigid → poor relaxation and filling.
- Leads to heart failure symptoms and rhythm problems.
- Cause often unknown, sometimes inherited.

### Arrhythmogenic Cardiomyopathy (ACM)
- Inherited; affects ventricles.
- Abnormal proteins weaken cell connections → muscle death replaced by scar/fatty tissue.
- Chambers thin and stretched → poor pumping.
- Causes arrhythmias, heart failure, sudden death in young athletes.
- Worsened by prolonged strenuous exercise.

### Broken Heart Syndrome (Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy)
- Temporary weakening of heart muscle due to extreme stress.
- Symptoms mimic heart attack (chest pain, breathlessness).
- More common in women; reversible and rarely recurs.

## Diagnosis
- ECG, echocardiogram, MRI, rhythm monitoring, exercise tests.
- Family history analysis.
- Genetic testing for inherited types; relatives may be screened.

## Treatments
- No cure, but symptoms can be managed.
- **Lifestyle changes:** healthy diet, gentle exercise, quit smoking, weight control, reduce alcohol, manage stress, treat underlying conditions.
- **Medicines:** blood pressure control, rhythm correction, diuretics, anticoagulants, beta-blockers.
- **Procedures:**
  - Septal reduction (alcohol injection or myectomy) for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
  - Arrhythmia ablation
  - Pacemaker or implantable cardioverter defibrillator (ICD)
  - Heart transplant (last resort)

## Notes
- Cardiomyopathy varies in severity; some mild cases controlled with lifestyle changes.
- Early diagnosis is crucial to prevent complications.
- Inherited forms require family screening.
- High risk due to potential for heart failure, arrhythmias, and sudden cardiac death.
