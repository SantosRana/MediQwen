# High Blood Pressure (Hypertension)

## Overview
- High blood pressure (hypertension) increases risk of heart attack, stroke, kidney disease, and vascular dementia.
- Very common, especially in older adults.
- Often has no symptoms — many people unaware they have it.

## Risk Factors
- Age (risk increases as you get older)
- Family history
- Ethnicity (higher risk in Black African, Black Caribbean, South Asian backgrounds)
- Unhealthy diet (especially high salt intake)
- Overweight or obesity
- Smoking
- Excess alcohol
- Long-term stress

## Symptoms
- Usually no symptoms.
- Rarely: headaches, blurred vision, chest pain.
- Only way to confirm is a blood pressure check.

## When to Seek Help
- **NHS 111:** if frequent headaches, blurred vision, chest pain, or other worrying symptoms.
- **999 / A&E:** if sudden chest pain that does not go away, pain spreading to arm/neck/jaw/back, chest pain with sweating, nausea, dizziness, or breathlessness (possible heart attack).

## Diagnosis
- Blood pressure test with cuff around upper arm.
- If high, may require:
  - Home monitoring
  - Repeat GP checks
  - 24-hour portable monitor

**Thresholds:**
- ≥140/90 mmHg (clinic)
- ≥135/85 mmHg (home)

## Risks
Untreated high blood pressure increases risk of:
- Heart disease
- Heart attack
- Stroke
- Heart failure
- Kidney disease
- Vascular dementia

## Prevention & Lifestyle
**Do:**
- Eat a healthy, balanced diet
- Exercise regularly (≥150 minutes/week)
- Lose weight if overweight

**Don’t:**
- Avoid excess salt
- Limit alcohol (<14 units/week)
- Limit caffeine (coffee, tea, cola)
- Do not smoke

**NHS Support:** Better Health tools for weight loss, quitting smoking, reducing alcohol, and increasing activity.

## Treatments
- **Medicines:** prescribed if blood pressure is very high, if risk factors present, or if lifestyle changes insufficient.
- Types vary by age, ethnicity, and severity.
- Often taken daily as tablets; sometimes more than one medicine needed.

## Notes
- Hypertension is a silent but serious condition.
- Regular checks are essential, especially for adults over 40.
- Lifestyle changes can significantly reduce risk.
- Medicines are effective but must be taken consistently.
