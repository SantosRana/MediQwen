# Low Blood Pressure (Hypotension)

## Overview
- Defined as a blood pressure reading <90/60 mmHg.
- Often harmless and may not cause symptoms.
- Treatment needed if symptoms occur or underlying cause identified.

## Symptoms
- Lightheadedness or dizziness
- Nausea
- Blurred vision
- Weakness
- Confusion
- Fainting

**Postural hypotension:** symptoms triggered when standing up or changing position suddenly.

## When to See a GP
- Persistent symptoms such as dizziness or fainting.
- GP may investigate underlying causes and recommend treatment.

## Diagnosis
Blood pressure can be checked:
- At a pharmacy
- By a GP or practice nurse
- At home with a monitor

**Threshold:** <90/60 mmHg indicates low blood pressure.  
Adults aged 40–74 should have blood pressure checked at least every 5 years.

## Treatment
- Address underlying cause (e.g., adjust medicines, treat conditions).
- Support stockings to improve circulation.
- Medicines rarely needed; lifestyle changes often sufficient.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Get up slowly from sitting/lying
- Move gradually when getting out of bed
- Eat small, frequent meals (rest after eating may help)
- Increase water intake

**Don’t:**
- Avoid standing/sitting for long periods
- Avoid sudden posture changes
- Limit alcohol intake

## Causes
Blood pressure varies with time of day, activity, and emotional state.  
Possible causes include:
- Genetic predisposition
- Ageing
- Pregnancy
- Medical conditions (e.g., diabetes)
- Certain medicines

## Notes
- Low blood pressure is often benign, especially in fit individuals.
- Persistent or symptomatic hypotension requires medical evaluation.
- Lifestyle adjustments usually control symptoms effectively.
