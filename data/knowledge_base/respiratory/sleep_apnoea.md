# Sleep Apnoea (Obstructive Sleep Apnoea - OSA)

## Overview
- Sleep apnoea is when breathing stops and starts during sleep.
- Most common type: **obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA)**.
- Needs treatment to prevent serious complications.

## Symptoms
**During sleep:**
- Breathing stops and starts
- Gasping, snorting, choking noises
- Waking up frequently
- Loud snoring

**During the day:**
- Excessive tiredness
- Poor concentration
- Mood swings
- Morning headaches

## When to Seek Help
- **GP:** if breathing stops/starts during sleep, gasping noises, or persistent daytime tiredness.
- Bring someone who has observed symptoms to appointment.

## Diagnosis
- Referral to sleep clinic.
- Overnight tests (breathing, heartbeat monitoring).
- May be done at home or in clinic.
- Severity measured by **AHI score** (apnoea-hypopnoea index).

## Treatments
- **Lifestyle changes:**
  - Weight loss
  - Stop smoking
  - Reduce alcohol
- **CPAP machine:**
  - Pumps air into mask worn during sleep
  - Keeps airways open
  - Improves sleep quality, reduces tiredness
  - Lowers risk of complications (e.g., high blood pressure)
- **Other treatments:**
  - Mandibular advancement device (gum shield)
  - Surgery (e.g., remove tonsils)
  - Mouth/facial muscle exercises
  - Hypoglossal nerve stimulation (electrical signals to tongue nerve)

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Lose weight if overweight
- Exercise regularly
- Maintain good sleep habits (dark, quiet room, regular schedule)
- Sleep on your side (tennis ball trick, special pillow/wedge)

**Don’t:**
- Do not smoke
- Limit alcohol (especially before sleep)
- Avoid sleeping pills unless prescribed

## Complications
Untreated sleep apnoea increases risk of:
- High blood pressure
- Stroke
- Type 2 diabetes
- Heart disease
- Depression/mood changes
- Serious accidents due to tiredness
- Difficulty concentrating at work/school
- Relationship strain due to disrupted sleep

## Driving and Sleep Apnoea
- Must inform DVLA if diagnosed with sleep apnoea and excessive sleepiness.
- Do not drive until symptoms are controlled.

## Causes
- Airways become too narrow during sleep.
- Risk factors:
  - Obesity
  - Large neck
  - Age (though children/young adults can be affected)
  - Family history
  - Alcohol
  - Smoking
  - Large tonsils/adenoids
  - Sleeping on back
  - COPD

## Notes
- Sleep apnoea is common but often undiagnosed.
- CPAP is highly effective if used consistently.
- Lifestyle changes can improve mild cases.
