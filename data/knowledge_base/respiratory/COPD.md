# Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

## Overview
- COPD is a group of lung conditions causing breathing difficulties.
- Includes **emphysema** (damage to air sacs) and **chronic bronchitis** (long-term airway inflammation).
- Common in middle-aged or older adults, especially smokers.
- Breathing problems worsen gradually, limiting daily activities.
- Treatment helps control symptoms but cannot reverse lung damage.

## Symptoms
- Shortness of breath (especially when active)
- Persistent chesty cough with phlegm (“smoker’s cough”)
- Frequent chest infections
- Persistent wheezing
- Symptoms worsen over time; flare-ups (exacerbations) may occur suddenly.

## When to Seek Help
- See GP if persistent symptoms, especially if >35 and smoke or used to smoke.
- Early treatment prevents further lung damage.

## Causes
- Lung inflammation, damage, narrowing.
- **Main cause:** smoking (risk increases with duration and amount).
- Other causes:
  - Long-term exposure to harmful fumes/dust
  - Rare genetic condition (alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency)

## Diagnosis
- GP assessment: symptoms, smoking history.
- Breathing tests (spirometry).
- Rule out other conditions (e.g., asthma).

## Treatments
- **Lifestyle:**
  - Stop smoking (most important step).
- **Medicines:**
  - Inhalers and tablets to ease breathing.
- **Pulmonary rehabilitation:**
  - Exercise and education programme.
- **Surgery/lung transplant:**
  - Rare, for severe cases only.

## Outlook
- COPD cannot be cured or reversed.
- Treatment helps many people maintain daily activities.
- In some, COPD worsens despite treatment, leading to life-threatening complications.

## Prevention
- Largely preventable by avoiding smoking.
- Stopping smoking slows progression and prevents further damage.

## Notes
- COPD is a major cause of respiratory disability worldwide.
- Early diagnosis and smoking cessation are critical for long-term outcomes.
