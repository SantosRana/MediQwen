# Lung Cancer

## Overview
- One of the most common and serious cancers.
- ~43,000 people diagnosed annually in the UK.
- Often no symptoms in early stages; symptoms develop as disease progresses.
- Two main types: **non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC)** and **small-cell lung cancer (SCLC)**.

## Symptoms
- Persistent cough (lasting >3 weeks or worsening)
- Coughing up blood
- Persistent breathlessness
- Chest pain when breathing/coughing
- Frequent chest infections
- Fatigue, loss of appetite, unexplained weight loss
- Less common: finger clubbing, dysphagia, wheezing, hoarse voice, facial/neck swelling, shoulder pain

## Types
- **NSCLC:** ~80–85% of cases (squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, large-cell carcinoma)
- **SCLC:** less common, spreads faster

## Causes
- **Smoking:** main risk factor (>70% of cases).  
  - Cigarettes, cigars, pipe tobacco, chewing tobacco, snuff.  
  - Cannabis (often mixed with tobacco) may also increase risk.
- **Passive smoking**
- **Radon gas exposure**
- **Occupational exposures:** asbestos, arsenic, beryllium, cadmium, coal/coke fumes, silica, nickel, diesel fumes
- **Genetic mutations** in cancer cells

## Diagnosis
- GP assessment: symptoms, smoking history.
- Tests: chest X-ray, CT scan, biopsy, bronchoscopy.
- Staging determines spread and guides treatment.

## Treatments
- **NSCLC:**
  - Surgery (lobectomy, pneumonectomy, wedge resection/segmentectomy)
  - Radiotherapy (sometimes combined with chemotherapy)
  - Chemotherapy
  - Immunotherapy
  - Targeted/biological therapies (for specific mutations)
- **SCLC:**
  - Chemotherapy (main treatment)
  - Radiotherapy or immunotherapy (sometimes combined)
  - Surgery only in very early cases
- **Supportive care:** symptom relief, palliative care

## Outlook
- Prognosis depends on type, stage, and overall health.
- Early diagnosis improves survival chances.
- Advanced disease often requires combined therapies.

## Prevention
- **Stop smoking:** best way to reduce risk.  
  - Risk decreases significantly after quitting; after 15 years, risk approaches that of non-smokers.
- **Healthy lifestyle:** balanced diet (low-fat, high-fibre, fruit/veg, wholegrains), regular exercise.
- **Avoid occupational exposures** and passive smoking.

## Notes
- Lung cancer is a leading cause of cancer death worldwide.
- Early detection and smoking cessation are critical.
- Managed by a multidisciplinary cancer team (oncologists, surgeons, nurses).
