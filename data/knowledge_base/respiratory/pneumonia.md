# Pneumonia

## Overview
- Pneumonia is inflammation of the lungs, usually caused by infection.
- Most people recover in 2–4 weeks.
- Babies, older adults, and those with heart or lung conditions are at higher risk of severe illness and may need hospital care.

## Symptoms
- Cough (yellow/green mucus)
- Shortness of breath
- High temperature
- Chest pain
- Aching body
- Fatigue
- Loss of appetite
- Wheezing (babies may grunt)
- Confusion (common in older adults)

## When to Seek Help
- **Urgent GP/111:**  
  - Cough >3 weeks  
  - Coughing blood  
  - Chest pain (on/off, or when breathing/coughing)  
  - Shortness of breath  

- **999 / A&E:**  
  - Struggling to breathe (choking, gasping, unable to speak)  
  - Pale/blue/blotchy skin, lips, tongue  
  - Sudden confusion  
  - Baby unresponsive/floppy  

## Treatments
- Antibiotics (most recover in 2–4 weeks).
- Hospital care if:
  - Age >65
  - Cardiovascular or chronic lung condition
  - Baby/young child affected
  - Severe illness or poor response to antibiotics
- Hospital treatment: fluids, antibiotics, steroids, oxygen, chest X-ray, blood tests.

**Important:** Contact doctor if no improvement after 2–3 days of antibiotics.

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Rest until better
- Drink plenty of fluids
- Take paracetamol/ibuprofen for pain/fever
- Cover mouth/nose when coughing/sneezing
- Dispose of tissues promptly
- Wash hands regularly

**Don’t:**
- Do not take cough medicine (coughing clears infection)
- Do not smoke

## Causes
- Usually bacterial or viral infection.
- Can follow other infections: flu, RSV, COVID-19.
- Rarely fungal (in healthy UK adults).
- Aspiration pneumonia: caused by food/water entering lungs.

## Prevention
- Vaccines:
  - Pneumococcal (babies, adults >65, high-risk groups)
  - Flu (pregnancy, adults >65, chronic conditions, high-risk groups)
  - RSV (adults 75–79, pregnancy from 28 weeks)
  - COVID-19 (seasonal, for at-risk groups)
- Stop smoking to reduce risk.

## Notes
- Pneumonia is common but can be serious in vulnerable groups.
- Early treatment and vaccination reduce complications.
- Hygiene and lifestyle measures help prevent spread.
