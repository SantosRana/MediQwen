# Allergic Rhinitis

## Symptoms
Common symptoms (similar to a cold) include:
- Sneezing
- Itchy nose
- Runny or blocked nose
- Itchy, red, watery eyes
- Cough
- Itchy roof of mouth

Symptoms usually occur within minutes of allergen exposure.

## Causes
Allergic rhinitis is caused by an allergic reaction. Common allergens:
- Pollen (trees, grass, weeds → hay fever)
- House dust mites
- Animal dander (cats, dogs)
- Mould
- Wood dust, flour dust, latex

Family history of allergies, eczema, or asthma increases risk.  
Different from **non-allergic rhinitis** (caused by colds, temperature, humidity).

## Self Care
- Avoid known triggers where possible
- Pharmacy medicines:
  - Antihistamines
  - Decongestant sprays/drops (not for children <6)
  - Steroid nasal sprays
  - Saline sprays/solutions
- Do not use nasal sprays long-term (can worsen symptoms)
- Homemade saline rinse can help
- Rest at home if fever or feeling unwell

## When to See a Doctor
See a GP if:
- Symptoms worsen
- You also have asthma that is worsening
- Symptoms affect sleep or daily life
- Cause of symptoms is unclear
- Pharmacy treatments are not effective

## GP Treatments
- Prescription steroid nasal sprays or antihistamines
- Referral to specialist if severe or unclear cause

## Prevention
**Do:**
- Wear wraparound sunglasses (reduce pollen exposure)
- Use synthetic bedding, wash at ≥60°C
- Dust with damp cloth, vacuum with HEPA filter
- Wash pets regularly, groom outdoors
- Wash pet bedding on hot cycle
- Keep home dry, ventilated, manage damp/condensation

**Don’t:**
- Allow pets in bedrooms
- Go outside or dry clothes outdoors when pollen count is high

## Notes
- Allergic rhinitis is common and manageable
- Trigger avoidance + pharmacy medicines usually effective
- Severe cases may need specialist care
