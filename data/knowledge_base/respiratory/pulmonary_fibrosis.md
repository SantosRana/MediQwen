# Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF)

## Overview
- IPF is a condition where lungs become scarred, making breathing increasingly difficult.
- Cause is unknown (“idiopathic”).
- Usually affects people aged 70–75; rare under 50.
- No cure; treatments slow progression but cannot reverse scarring.

## Symptoms
- Gradual onset, worsening over time:
  - Shortness of breath
  - Persistent dry cough
  - Fatigue
  - Loss of appetite, weight loss
  - Clubbed fingers (rounded, swollen fingertips)
- Breathlessness may eventually occur even with light activity.

## When to Seek Help
- GP if persistent breathing difficulties or cough >3 weeks.
- Symptoms are not normal and should be investigated.

## Diagnosis
- Hospital referral for tests:
  - Lung function (breathing) tests
  - Blood tests
  - Chest X-ray, CT scan
  - Lung biopsy (via keyhole surgery)

## Causes
- Alveoli (air sacs) become damaged and scarred → stiff lungs, reduced oxygen transfer.
- Exact cause unknown.
- Linked factors:
  - Dust exposure (metal, wood)
  - Viral infections
  - Family history (~1 in 20 cases)
  - Acid reflux
  - Smoking

## Treatments
- **Self care:** stop smoking, eat healthily, exercise regularly.
- **Medicines:** pirfenidone, nintedanib (slow scarring progression).
- **Oxygen therapy:** mask at home or outside.
- **Pulmonary rehabilitation:** exercises and advice to ease breathing.
- **Lung transplant:** rare, limited by donor availability.

## Outlook
- IPF worsens over time, but progression speed varies.
- Some remain relatively symptom-free for years; others deteriorate quickly.
- Possible complications: chest infections, pulmonary hypertension, heart failure.
- Prognosis difficult to predict at diagnosis; regular monitoring essential.

## Notes
- Support available from UK charities (Action for Pulmonary Fibrosis, Pulmonary Fibrosis Trust).
- Research ongoing into better treatments.
