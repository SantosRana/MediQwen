# Asthma

## Overview
- Asthma is a common long-term condition affecting breathing.
- No cure, but well-managed asthma usually causes few problems.
- Symptoms vary in severity and can be triggered by allergens, pollution, cold air, or exercise.
- Can start in childhood or at any age.

## Symptoms
- Wheezing
- Coughing
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness
- Symptoms worse at night or early morning
- Asthma attack: severe symptoms, difficulty breathing (life-threatening)

## When to Seek Help
- **GP:** if symptoms present, worsening, or interfering with daily life.
- **Urgent GP/111:** after recovering from an asthma attack (within 2 days).
- **999 / A&E:** if asthma attack does not improve with inhaler, or no inhaler available.

## Asthma Attack Management
- Sit upright, stay calm.
- Use reliever inhaler:
  - Blue inhaler: 1 puff every 30–60 seconds, max 10 puffs.
  - AIR/MART inhaler: 1 puff every 1–3 minutes, max 6 puffs.
- Follow personal asthma action plan if different.
- Call 999 if symptoms worsen or do not improve.

## Diagnosis
- GP/nurse assessment, chest exam.
- Breathing tests, blood tests.
- Peak flow meter monitoring at home.
- Diagnosis may take time due to variable symptoms.

## Treatments
- Care team: GP, nurse, sometimes specialist.
- Asthma action plan provided and updated annually.
- **Inhalers:**
  - Anti-inflammatory reliever (AIR) inhaler
  - Maintenance and reliever therapy (MART) inhaler
  - Preventer inhaler + blue reliever inhaler
- Correct inhaler use essential (different types: dry powder, pMDI, soft mist).
- Return inhalers to pharmacy for safe disposal.
- **Other medicines:**
  - Stronger inhalers or tablets (e.g., montelukast)
  - Biological therapies (injections) for severe asthma
- **Hospital treatment for severe attacks:**
  - Oxygen mask
  - Nebuliser
  - Steroid tablets/injections

## Self Care
**Do:**
- Always carry inhaler
- Use inhalers as prescribed
- Avoid triggers (pollution, smoke, pollen, animals)
- Exercise regularly (with medical advice if exercise triggers symptoms)
- Maintain healthy weight
- Get vaccinations (e.g., flu)

**Don’t:**
- Do not smoke
- Avoid medicines that may trigger asthma (aspirin, ibuprofen, beta blockers) unless approved by doctor

## Life Impact
- Often lifelong, but may resolve in children.
- Severity varies; well-managed asthma has minimal impact.
- Severe asthma requires additional treatments.
- Asthma attacks can be life-threatening without proper management.

## Asthma and Pregnancy
- Important to manage asthma during pregnancy for maternal and fetal health.
- Inform doctor or midwife about asthma.

## Causes
- Airways become swollen and narrow.
- Triggers: allergies, exercise, smoke, cold air, infections.
- Risk factors:
  - Allergies (eczema, hay fever, food allergies)
  - Family history of asthma/allergies
  - Premature birth or low birth weight
  - Childhood exposure to pollution or smoke
  - Childhood respiratory illnesses (bronchiolitis, croup)
  - Overweight
  - Occupational exposure to chemicals

## Notes
- Asthma requires lifelong monitoring and treatment.
- Inhalers are the cornerstone of management.
- Action plans and regular check-ups reduce risk of severe attacks.
