# Tuberculosis (TB)

## Overview
- TB is an infection usually affecting the lungs.
- Can be serious if untreated, but curable with antibiotics.
- A vaccine (BCG) helps protect people at higher risk.

## Symptoms
**Active TB (gradual onset):**
- Cough >3 weeks (with mucus or blood)
- Fatigue, exhaustion
- High temperature, night sweats
- Loss of appetite, weight loss
- General malaise
- Children: poor growth/weight gain

**If TB spreads beyond lungs:**
- Swollen glands
- Body aches, joint swelling
- Abdominal/pelvic pain
- Constipation
- Dark/cloudy urine
- Headache, vomiting, confusion, stiff neck
- Rash (legs, face, other areas)

**Latent TB:**  
- No symptoms, but bacteria present.  
- Can progress to active TB later.

## When to Seek Help
- **GP:** cough >3 weeks, unexplained fatigue, persistent fever/night sweats, weight loss, exposure to TB.
- **Urgent GP/111:** coughing blood.
- **999 / A&E:** stiff neck + severe headache, painful light sensitivity, seizures, sudden confusion, weakness/loss of movement (possible TB meningitis).

## Diagnosis
- Imaging: chest X-ray, ultrasound, echocardiogram, CT scan.
- Sputum (mucus) samples.
- Biopsy of affected tissue.
- **Latent TB tests:** Mantoux skin test, blood tests.

## Treatments
- **Active TB:** antibiotics for ≥6 months.
- **Latent TB:** antibiotics for 3–6 months.
- Steroids if TB affects brain, spinal cord, or heart lining.
- **Important:** complete full antibiotic course to prevent recurrence.

## Causes
- Caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* bacteria.
- Spread via droplets from coughing/sneezing of people with active TB.
- Latent TB cannot spread but may become active later.

## Risk Factors
- Close contact with active TB cases
- Born/lived in high-prevalence areas
- Weakened immune system (HIV, transplant, chemotherapy)
- Age <5
- Overcrowded/unhealthy living conditions (e.g., homelessness)
- Smoking, alcohol, drug use
- Previous TB not properly treated

## Vaccination
- **BCG vaccine** recommended for:
  - Babies in high-prevalence UK areas
  - Babies/children living with TB cases
  - Babies/children born in or with family from high-prevalence countries
  - People ≤35 spending >3 months in high-prevalence countries
  - At-risk workers (e.g., healthcare staff)

## Notes
- TB remains a global health challenge.
- Early diagnosis and adherence to treatment are critical.
- Vaccination and public health measures reduce risk.
